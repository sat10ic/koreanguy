## Key Takeaways
- **Trend Following Discipline:** The primary law of trend following is that you must accept giving back a portion of your profits at the end of a trend. Trying to exit at the absolute top is a trap that leads to premature exits and missed trends.
- **Simplicity and 'Dumb' Execution:** Over-analyzing charts and trying to time breakouts using subjectivity often backfires. Successful execution requires being 'dumb' enough to rigidly follow predefined rules.
- **Differentiate Momentum Styles:**
  - *Absolute Momentum:* High-velocity, explosive stocks that require tight, active risk management (trailing 10/20 EMA).
  - *Persistent Momentum:* Slow, steady uptrends that follow longer-term moving averages (20/50/100 EMA) and require wider stops and room to breathe.
- **AmiBroker & Pine Screener Setup:** Screen the universe using persistent moving average criteria (above 10 EMA for >= 20 days, 20 EMA for >= 30 days, 50 EMA for >= 50 days). Sort the results by **Average Daily Range (ADR)** to find explosive momentum within clean trends.
- **Pullbacks Over Breakouts:** Working professionals should avoid buying breakouts, which often lead to late, expensive entries. Instead, buy pullbacks to the 20 or 50 EMA on the downside.
- **Pyramiding and Portfolio Sizing:** Risk a maximum of 1% of the portfolio per trade. Start with small initial positions and build up to a 30% allocation through pyramiding as the stock moves in your favor.
- **Wealth Creation vs. Activity:** Swing trading (seeking 20-40% moves) is mentally draining and often results in getting shaken out. Positional trading (trailing the 50 DMA) is passive, requires minimal screen time, and captures 350-500% moves.

### Chapter 2: Sizing, Ignorance, and Trading Intelligence vs. Analysis
**Interval:** `[13:06 - 27:52]`

#### Chapter Summary
The discussion covers why over-analyzing and attempting to predict trend endings backfires in execution. The speakers explain that successful trading requires being 'dumb' enough to follow rules objectively, and show how understanding structural story 'buckets' (e.g., regulatory approvals, turnarounds, solar sector trends) provides the conviction needed to hold size.

#### Key Points & Strategy Rules
- **The Intelligence Trap:** Being highly intellectual or subjective works well for analysis but is counter-productive in trading execution. Standardized, rule-based simplicity ('playing dumb') is far more profitable.
- **Quantifying Fear:** Assigning a specific dollar or rupee amount to what you can lose from the peak stops you from constantly predicting the trend's end.
- **Catalyst Buckets:** You don't need to know every microscopic detail of a company's fundamentals. Instead, categorize stocks into story 'buckets' (e.g., USFDA approvals, turnaround plays like Zomato/Paytm, sector tailwinds like Solar).
