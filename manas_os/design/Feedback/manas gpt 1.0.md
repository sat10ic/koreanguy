## Entry architecture that earns the right to alert

The single best change is to replace the current readiness score with a **hierarchical gate-and-rank tree**. A stock must pass every mandatory gate before it can even appear as a candidate; only then do you sort the survivors. This avoids the additive-score path where mediocre names pile up enough small positives to look “A+.” The mandatory gates should be: **regime allowed**, **sector/industry leadership present**, **fresh-leg or event-origin present**, **risk-valid stop present**, **mechanics-safe name**, and **liquidity/slippage acceptable**. If any one fails, the tool should show the reason for refusal, not a lower score. The single number on the feed should be an **ordinal rank among passed names today**, not a synthetic 0–100 “confidence” score. That respects the rules-first and anti-mashup constraints while still giving one ranked number per screen. fileciteturn2file0

The highest-precision entry family for your stack is a **regime-gated episodic pivot acceptance trade**. **Mechanism:** require a real event or operating acceleration, then demand that the market accepts the news rather than merely reacting to it. Use ChartsMaze episodic-pivot, order-win, and announcement feeds, plus QoQ/YoY EPS growth, sales growth, and OPM growth. For an earnings-style EP, require at minimum: positive catalyst present; EPS growth and sales growth both strong on the latest quarter; sector RS in the top leadership bucket; stock RS already strong; no ASM flag; not extended beyond a fresh-leg threshold; and next-day opening acceptance. For opening acceptance, do not buy a raw gap. Use the regular pre-open only as context, then wait for the live market: NSE’s regular pre-open runs from 09:00 to 09:08 and the normal market opens at 09:15. A buy-trigger should require the first 15-minute range to hold above either the prior close or the midpoint of the gap, with price reclaiming or holding the opening-range high. If more than half the gap is filled in the first 15–30 minutes, treat it as gap rejection and downgrade to watch-only. citeturn13view0turn12view0

**Fields:** ChartsMaze `episodic_pivot`, `order_wins`, `announcements`, `eps_growth_qoq`, `eps_growth_yoy`, `sales_growth_qoq`, `sales_growth_yoy`, `opm_growth`; stock `rs_rating`; sector and industry RS; RRG quadrant/trend; daily OHLCV; delivery %; Fyers 1-minute and 5-minute candles; ASM flag. **Explainability:** every piece of evidence is a visible chip: “Catalyst present,” “Sector leading,” “Gap accepted,” “Held OR low,” “Fresh-leg, not extended,” “Stop valid.” **Validation:** run an event study on all historical EP/disclosure tags, then divide into acceptance versus rejection cohorts using next-day or same-day opening behavior. Evaluate T+5, T+10, and T+20 returns, but also whether the name reaches +2R before -1R. If you lack historical intraday retention, you can still run a daily close-to-close event study first, then forward-test the opening-acceptance subrule live for 8–12 weeks before promoting it. **Metric:** the load-bearing metric is **push-alert precision**: percentage of push alerts that reach +2R before -1R within 10 sessions. Also track T+10 excess return over the liquid universe median. fileciteturn2file0

The second entry family is **fresh-leg continuation after controlled contraction**. This is how you separate a 60% candidate from a 40% candidate in practice: you do not buy broad momentum, you buy momentum that is early in a new leg and cheap in stop-distance terms. **Mechanism:** require prior leadership, then buy either a tight base breakout or a first pullback after a valid breakout. “Fresh-leg” should mean several things at once: the setup is still close to its pivot or recent breakout; the base is not too deep; the stock is not already far above the 10/21 EMA; sector leadership is still intact; and the invalidation stop is tight. A practical initial rule: only allow technical breakouts when the stock is within roughly 5% of the pivot pre-trigger and when the required stop is within the setup’s stop cap. For pullbacks, require that the first retracement into the 10 EMA or 21 EMA happens on lower volume and narrower true range, then reclaims the prior day’s high or opening-range high. This is the opposite of “buying strength because everything is green.” It is buying **leadership surviving a pause**. fileciteturn2file0

**Fields:** ChartsMaze screeners for VCP, tight setup, Launch Pad, momentum, shakeout, pocket-pivot, EMA reclaim; daily OHLCV; ATR computed from daily bar data; volume; delivery %; RS rating; sector RS and RRG; Fyers intraday candles. **Explainability:** the evidence list is visual and beginner-friendly: “Tight base present,” “volume dried up,” “21 EMA held,” “sector still leading,” “ranked because stop is 4.2%, not 8.9%.” **Validation:** use a walk-forward test by month or quarter. On each signal day, compare the gated cohort with a looser “all technical setups” cohort. The edge is real only if the gated cohort has materially better +2R-before--1R probability, shallower median MAE, and better T+10 median return. **Metric:** track **MAE efficiency** and **R-multiple hit rate**: median maximum adverse excursion before success, and percentage of entries that deliver at least +2R before final stop-out. fileciteturn2file0

The third entry family, lower frequency but high value in India, is **disclosure-follow-through in under-covered names**. The key is to avoid pretending you have analyst-surprise data; you do not. The informational edge is not the existence of the disclosure; free sources show that. The edge is the rule set that decides whether the first and second day behavior implies accumulation or operator froth. This family should be stricter than ordinary chart setups: if the disclosure is positive but the name fails acceptance, loses the day-low, or prints suspicious volume with weak delivery structure, it should be formally rejected rather than left on screen as “interesting.” fileciteturn2file0

Your Telegram workflow should be built around **push scarcity**. Push only when a name has passed every mandatory gate **and** live confirmation has occurred. What measurably separates a 60%+ setup from a 40% setup in this market is not “more confluence.” It is six specific differences: the market regime allows that setup family; the sector is leading rather than merely bouncing; the stock is in a fresh leg rather than visibly extended; there is either a catalyst or a very clear contraction-and-reclaim structure; the stop is efficient; and the name is free of major mechanics hazards such as ASM, low price-band stress, or pump signatures. ## Risk rules that force valid trades and reject the rest

The risk engine should stop being advisory and become a **hard validator**. The first rule is a stop-placement hierarchy. **Mechanism:** for every candidate, compute three candidate invalidation stops and use the **tightest valid** one, not the tightest possible one: structural low; event low or gap low; and ATR-buffered stop below the relevant structure. A valid stop is one that sits beyond the setup’s invalidation, not one that is merely visually close. For EPs, the default invalidation is usually the low of the event day or the opening-acceptance low. For fresh-leg breakouts, the default invalidation is below the pivot shelf, undercut low, or 21 EMA support, with a small ATR buffer. For pullbacks, the low of the pullback plus buffer is the default. If no valid stop exists inside your maximum stop cap, the setup fails. fileciteturn2file0

The second rule is a **maximum acceptable stop-distance schedule**, and this is where the current 27% nonsense dies. My build recommendation is straightforward. For large and very liquid names, cap the initial stop at roughly **3.5% to 5.5%**. For liquid midcaps, **5% to 6.5%**. For smallcaps with a genuine catalyst and clean mechanics, **6% to 8%**, but only if all other filters are unusually strong. Above **8%**, the default should be refusal unless the trade is explicitly categorized as a special-situation event with an unusually small pilot size and strong acceptance behavior. In normal swing workflow, anything above that is not a swing trade; it is a thesis placement pretending to be a plan. The fact that your current setup feed can show a 27% stop is itself evidence that stop validity must become a gate, not a description. fileciteturn2file0

**Fields:** daily OHLCV; ATR14 from daily bars; recent swing lows/highs derived from price structure; Fyers intraday low/high for opening-range events; market cap proxy; rupee turnover computed from close × volume; ASM flag; recent price-band-hit state if ingested; price. **Explainability:** the app should literally show: “stop chosen = event-day low – 0.25 ATR,” or “stop chosen = prior shelf low – 0.2 ATR,” and “rejected because required stop = 9.4%, max allowed in this bucket = 6.5%.” **Validation:** historical replay of all candidate setups, comparing fixed-percentage stops versus hierarchy stops versus invalidation-plus-ATR stops. The stop logic wins only if it reduces both median loss size and premature stopouts measured by subsequent +2R successes. **Metric:** **stop validity ratio** = percentage of stopped trades that never subsequently trade above the original trigger by +1R. Also track median realized loss as a percent of planned loss. fileciteturn2file0

Position sizing should be fully mechanical once the stop is valid: `position size = equity × account-risk% ÷ stop%`, then clipped by liquidity and regime caps. The missing part in most retail tools is the **liquidity override**. For a single-user manual account, you still need a cap based on cash turnover and spread risk. A clean rule is to cap intended position value at a low fraction of recent median rupee turnover and to refuse names where the expected position would be a visibly material fraction of normal liquidity. This matters more in India because the same name can be “tradable” on daily volume but still become awkward under circuit pressure or sparse opening liquidity. fileciteturn2file0turn26view2

Regime-adjusted capital risk should be explicit and enforced. In **RISK_ON**, allow roughly **0.50%–0.75%** account risk per position, maximum open risk around **2.5%–3.0%**. In **SELECTIVE**, **0.25%–0.50%** risk per position, max open risk around **1.5%–2.0%**. In **DEFENSIVE**, **0.10%–0.25%** only, often pilot-size only. In **NO_TRADE**, no new swing entries. These are not cosmetic labels; they should directly set the sizer defaults, the maximum number of positions, and whether new alerts can even be pushed. Your own state document already says the regime page currently suggests discipline but fails to enforce it downstream. That contradiction must end. fileciteturn2file0

Pyramiding should exist, but only as a **pullback add after proof**, never as a strength-chasing or loss-averaging device. Add only after the initial unit has reached at least +1R open profit, the stop on the combined position can be tightened without increasing account risk beyond the regime cap, and the add occurs on a constructive pullback or new base, not on a straight vertical extension. One-half size for the first add, one-third for the second, and never more than two adds. If the add requires a looser total stop than the original position, it is invalid. That rule is explainable and hard to misuse.

The India-specific refusal rules should be strict. NSE’s surveillance framework is not just a label; it changes the risk physics. ASM and GSM actions can include reduced price bands, periodic call auctions, transfer to trade-for-trade, 100% margins, additional surveillance deposits, and once-a-week trading in higher GSM stages; GSM Stage IV can even impose “no upward movement.” NSE’s current ASM framework explicitly shortlists securities using market-behavior variables including **delivery percentage**, client concentration, close-to-close movement, volume variation, market cap, and PE. citeturn28view2turn27view2turn34view0

That means the tool should **refuse to log or alert** any trade when one of these conditions holds: current ASM flag; official GSM status if you ingest it; price band already reduced to stress levels; recent repeated price-band hits; opening liquidity too weak relative to intended size; or strong pump signature. This is especially justified because long-term ASM criteria explicitly flag names with extreme volume variation, concentrated client participation, and low average delivery, and heavily restricted low-band names can be placed directly into Stage IV of long-term ASM. If you cannot ingest full GSM logic because GSM relies on fundamentals you do not possess, do not fake a proxy; ingest the official public GSM list instead. That is public data and far safer than inference. citeturn31view0turn33view0turn26view3

## Exit and profit-maintenance architecture

You do not compound by finding more winners. You compound by **keeping enough of each winner while keeping losers small**. The cleanest implementation is a **phase-based trailing system** rather than one universal stop. In the **early phase** of a new trend or event move, use the looser of: event-day low, 21 EMA with buffer, or structure stop. The job in that phase is not to “protect profits”; it is to avoid being shaken out of a move that has not yet had time to trend. In the **middle phase**, once the trade has reached roughly +1.5R to +2R and the 10 EMA has meaningfully caught up, switch to a somewhat tighter structure using either the 10 EMA or the last higher low. In the **late or extended phase**, especially after climactic volume or acceleration far above the 10 EMA, switch to a tighter exit regime and allow partial profit-taking into strength. This is more robust than a single ATR or single EMA rule applied regardless of trend maturity.

**Mechanism:** for each open trade, classify phase using simple, visible conditions: days since trigger, open R-multiple, distance from 10 EMA in ATR units, and whether the setup was event-driven or purely technical. Event-driven and fresh-leg trend trades begin on the looser trail; extended breakouts and third-stage continuation moves begin on the tighter trail. **Fields:** entry date and price; current close; 10 EMA; 21 EMA; ATR14; recent swing structure; volume; distribution-day count from daily bars; Fyers intraday reversal data if available. **Explainability:** “Phase = early trend, so trail = 21 EMA buffer,” or “Phase = extended, 2.3 ATR above 10 EMA, so partial and tighter trail.” **Validation:** replay all historical trades and compare fixed 21 EMA, fixed 10 EMA, ATR-only, and phase-based trails. **Metric:** **winner retention ratio** = realized R divided by peak MFE in R. If your winners routinely realize less than half of their peak MFE, your trail is too loose or your partial logic too poor.

The partial-booking rule should be asymmetric. **Sell into weakness on a new trend; sell into strength on extension.** In practice, that means early-phase winners should not be aggressively partially sold just because they are green. Instead, you should hold the core and only exit on actual deterioration. Once a position becomes extended, though, especially after wide-range up days, gap-ups far above the 10 EMA, or vertical moves into known circuit-sensitive behavior, partials become sensible. A practical rule is to allow the first partial only after at least +2R open profit or after a clear extension threshold, such as price stretching above the 10 EMA by a fixed ATR multiple. Anything earlier usually turns a real trend engine into a nervous profit-harvester.

Exit-signal composites should remain binary and inspectable. I would use a **two-strike exit composite**: exit or materially de-risk when any two of the following occur within a short window: close below the active trail; downside reversal day on clearly elevated volume; two distribution days in five sessions; sector RS rolls from leading/improving into weakening while the broader regime also downgrades; or post-event failure below the event-day midpoint. That is explainable. It also avoids the mess of parallel exit engines and conflicting opinions, which your current state file correctly identifies as credibility poison. fileciteturn2file0

Portfolio-level drawdown control matters more than any single stop rule. A single-user swing book should have a **maximum open-risk budget**, a **sector concentration budget**, and a **loss-pause rule**. The buildable version is simple. Cap open portfolio risk by regime as above; cap gross exposure to any one sector at roughly one-third of equity or, more conservatively, two names unless the regime is fully risk-on and the sector is the clear leadership group; and cut new position size in half after two full-R losses in a row or a weekly drawdown above a fixed threshold. A monthly drawdown guardrail should stop new risk from expanding until the regime improves. If your process never allows a large drawdown, compounding stops depending on hero trades.

## Regime as a hard governor, not a dashboard

The regime engine should become a **finite state machine with hard downstream consequences**: **RISK_ON**, **SELECTIVE**, **DEFENSIVE**, and **NO_TRADE**. You already have breadth and sector infrastructure, but the state file says the posture verdict is not being enforced downstream. That is the critical mistake. The feed width, allowed setup families, stop caps, default risk percentage, max open risk, max positions, and Telegram behavior should all change automatically by regime. A regime label without mechanical consequences is theatre. fileciteturn2file0

**Mechanism:** derive regime from four pillars: breadth/participation, index trend structure, volatility state, and sector leadership. Breadth can come from your existing NIFTYMIDSML400-derived breadth data. Trend structure can be computed from benchmark indices and from the cross-section itself, using percent of symbols above 20/50/200-day moving averages and the slope of those aggregates. Volatility does not require options or India VIX; if you do not ingest India VIX, use realized volatility proxies such as median ATR% across the tradable universe and rolling index return dispersion. Sector leadership comes directly from ChartsMaze sector/industry RS and RRG. The point is not elegance. The point is downstream enforceability. fileciteturn2file0

The actionable regime policy should be explicit. In **RISK_ON**, show more names, allow breakouts plus EPs plus first-pullback continuation, and permit normal risk caps. In **SELECTIVE**, allow only A-tier fresh-leg setups and accepted event trades; suppress late-stage breakouts and theme-chasing; reduce feed width to a small list. In **DEFENSIVE**, suppress ordinary breakout setups altogether and allow only pilot-size event trades in clear leadership sectors, or no new trades if breadth damage is broad. In **NO_TRADE**, show a curated watchlist for education only. The user should feel the regime not as a color but as the menu disappearing.

“Days like today” analog matching can be useful, but only as a **descriptive overlay** until you have deeper history. **Mechanism:** store each day as a feature vector of breadth, participation, cross-sectional ATR%, sector leadership concentration, and index trend state. Use nearest-neighbor analogs to find prior days with similar structure, then surface what happened to breadth, setup hit rates, and top-sector leadership at T+5 and T+10. **Explainability:** the app can literally show the five nearest past dates and the subsequent outcomes. **Validation:** use expanding-window walk-forward: only past analogs can be candidates; compare analog forecasts to unconditional regime outcomes. **Metric:** median analog outcome error and whether analog-based throttling actually reduces drawdown. **Theatre warning:** using analogs as hard gating with only a year or so of data is overfit theatre.

## Indian NSE market mechanics that are real edge

The most durable India-specific edge in your current stack is **public-disclosure drift in under-covered names**, especially small and midcaps, but only when paired with price acceptance and safety filters. The edge is structural because free sites surface announcements, but they do not usually convert them into a disciplined rule set that says: which disclosures matter, which ones still have drift potential, which ones are already too extended, and which ones are operator-risk traps. This is one of the rare places where a single-user, manual, public-data tool can still beat commodity dashboards because the alpha is in the **refusal logic after disclosure**, not in knowing that the disclosure exists. Your ChartsMaze feeds already include order wins, announcements, insider activity, bulk deals, circuit revisions, and episodic pivots; that is stronger than most free watchlists if you operationalize it. fileciteturn2file0

The second real edge is **surveillance-aware filtering**. NSE’s ASM framework explicitly uses delivery percentage, client concentration, volume variation, high-low variation, close-to-close price variation, market cap, unique PANs, and PE to shortlist suspicious securities, and the surveillance actions themselves can include reduced price bands, periodic call auction, and trade-for-trade. GSM goes even further with stage-based trading restrictions, 100% margins, surveillance deposits, once-a-week trading, and Stage IV “no upward movement.” Those are not academic curiosities; they directly alter tradability and stop reliability. A retail trader who simply refuses such names can avoid a large class of chart patterns that look powerful but are operationally toxic. citeturn28view2turn27view2turn34view0

The third real edge is **delivery-percentage used as a context filter, not as a standalone buy signal**. Two mistakes are common. One is to label high delivery as automatic accumulation; that is theatre. The other is to ignore delivery entirely. The correct use, in your stack, is comparative. Rising price plus strong volume plus delivery percentage above the stock’s own recent median is constructive. Violent volume expansion with persistently weak delivery, especially when paired with concentrated participation or recent circuits, is suspicious. NSE’s own ASM criteria give you a good reason to elevate this from folklore into a formal filter: a long-term ASM criterion explicitly combines enormous monthly volume variation, concentrated client participation, and average delivery below 50%, while providing an explicit bulk/block exemption when such trades account for a large share of average volume. That means the exchange itself treats the interaction of volume, delivery, and concentrated participation as surveillance-relevant. Your tool should too. citeturn31view0

The fourth real edge is **block/bulk-deal footprint interpretation**. The useful question is not “did a bulk/block trade happen?” The useful question is whether the trade explains the day’s volume and how price behaves after it. NSE publicly surfaces large-deal information, and India’s block-deal framework now runs in special windows with a higher minimum size threshold than before. But large deals are explanatory context, not automatic sponsorship. If a huge-volume day is mostly explained by a block or bulk event, downgrade the accumulation interpretation unless post-deal follow-through is positive and delivery structure stays healthy. If a stock advances with improving delivery and without needing deal-driven volume to explain the move, that is cleaner. citeturn14view1turn20news0

The fifth real edge is a **pump-signature exclusion filter** built specifically for Indian smallcaps. **Mechanism:** reject or heavily penalize names with any combination of these features: repeated price-band hits or circuit-to-circuit behavior; recent circuit-revision feed; ASM flag; abnormal one-month volume spike with weak delivery; extremely concentrated theme clustering; gap-ups that instantly fail; and low-band names with extreme annual variation. This is not anti-momentum. It is anti-fragile-momentum. **Fields:** daily OHLCV; price-band-hit history if ingested; circuit-revision feed; ASM/GSM official lists; bulk/block disclosures; delivery %; sector concentration. **Explainability:** every exclusion is visible and tied to a known market-mechanics risk. **Validation:** compare outcomes of accepted versus excluded smallcap event/momentum names. **Metric:** false-negative cost is acceptable here; what matters is reduction in severe adverse events, slippage blowouts, and names that cannot be exited near plan. **Theatre warning:** using “operator-driven” as a fuzzy story without explicit exclusion rules is theatre.

## The journal loop and the ranked build roadmap

The moat should be built as a **decision journal, not a trade diary**. Log every candidate that passed the gate, every candidate that nearly passed, every push alert, every manual override, every taken trade, and every skipped trade. For each one, store the full state available at decision time: regime, sector state, setup family, event type, gap status, stop width, surveillance state, liquidity bucket, human action, and later outcomes at T+5, T+10, T+20, max favorable excursion, max adverse excursion, and realized R if taken. The user’s private edge does not come from “remembering chart patterns.” It comes from building a private labeled dataset on how *their* filters, *their* regime definitions, and *their* discretionary overrides actually perform. That is exactly the moat your own state file says is currently missing. fileciteturn2file0

To avoid overfitting, the loop should be hierarchical and conservative. Never let a small sample in one narrow bucket wildly change future behavior. Use a **parent-child expectancy structure**: overall expectancy, then setup-family expectancy, then setup × regime expectancy, then setup × regime × mechanics-risk expectancy. Every child estimate should shrink back toward the parent until sample size is adequate. Freeze rules into versions, and only compare them in walk-forward “champion vs challenger” mode. The journal may inform ranking early, but it should not change gating or sizing until the sample is big enough. A clean minimum is **100 completed observations** before a setup family affects sizing, and at least **30 observations per setup × regime cell** before that cell can affect gating or size multipliers. If you do not have that, surface the read as “informative but not trusted.” That is how you turn a private loop into edge rather than superstition.

The minimum data before trusting different outputs should be explicit. **Alert-quality ranking:** maybe after 40–50 observations in one family, but only as a soft sort. **Sizing adjustments:** not before 100 completed instances and positive expectancy net of slippage. **Rule promotion into hard gating:** not before two distinct market regimes and at least one drawdown phase are represented. **Analog regime matching as a governor:** not before several hundred market days, ideally several years, unless it remains descriptive only. Right now, with the time span visible in your current ingestion, the loop should start collecting immediately but should be promoted cautiously. fileciteturn2file0

The highest-impact build roadmap, ranked, is this:

1. **Replace readiness with a gate tree and ordinal rank.** This is the single most important change because it turns the product from screener to opinion engine. Success metric: feed width collapses, push-alert precision rises, and rejected names underperform accepted names on a T+10 basis. fileciteturn2file0

2. **Promote risk validation to hard refusal logic.** No alert without a valid stop, valid R-math, valid liquidity, and safe mechanics. Success metric: zero alerts with absurd stop widths; lower realized loss variance; lower gap-through-stop damage. fileciteturn2file0

3. **Build event-acceptance entries using disclosures and EPs.** This is the best edge already latent in your dataset. Success metric: push-alert +2R-before--1R rate materially above ordinary chart setups. fileciteturn2file0

4. **Enforce regime downstream.** Let regime change feed size, allowed setup families, stop caps, line-up size, and alert behavior. Success metric: better expectancy and lower drawdown by regime; fewer bad trades taken during defensive periods. fileciteturn2file0

5. **Ingest official GSM and price-band/surveillance lists daily.** Do not infer fundamental surveillance logic you cannot observe; consume the official public lists. Success metric: fewer toxic names, fewer slippage and restriction events, fewer trades in impaired names. citeturn26view3turn26view2turn17view2

6. **Turn Telegram into a scarce execution layer.** Push only A-tier, live-confirmed names; digest the rest. Success metric: high open rate, high take rate on pushes, low spam fatigue, strong push-vs-digest outcome spread. fileciteturn2file0

7. **Build the journal→outcome→learnings loop before adding more detectors.** Success metric: every push and every manual pass is logged with later outcomes; expectancy starts to calibrate by family and regime. fileciteturn2file0
