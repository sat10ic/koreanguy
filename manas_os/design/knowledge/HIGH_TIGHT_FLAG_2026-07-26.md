## 1. What the pattern is — definition + the numbers

**Consensus core** (all sources agree on the shape, cluster on the same magnitudes):
- A near-vertical prior run-up (the "pole"), then a shallow sideways/down consolidation
  (the "flag"), then a fresh breakout.
- Pole duration: 4–8 weeks (**CC** "at least 90 percent two months or less"; **IN** "4 to 8
  weeks"; **SH** "less than circa 8 weeks").
- Flag/consolidation duration: 3–5 weeks (**CC**, **TL1**, **IN** all state "3 to 5 weeks" /
  "3, 4, or 5 weeks").

**Where sources disagree — three numbers, shown side by side, not averaged:**

**Disagreement #1 — minimum pole rise (the floor).**
- **CC**: "at least 90 percent" in ≤8 weeks, "100 percent more preferred." A 90% floor with
  100%+ as the better case.
- **TL1**: "you want to trade around that 100% rise" — no 90% floor stated, targets 100%.
- **IN**: "100 to 120%" — explicit floor of 100%, no 90% allowance mentioned.
- **SH**: "you'd like to see the stock go up over 100%" — same 100%+ floor as IN.
- Net: only CC (the O'Neil/Soreide originator's own looser framing) admits a 90% pole;
  IN and SH both cluster on 100%+ as the entry criterion.

**Disagreement #2 — flag/pullback depth ceiling.**
- **CC / TL1** (Soreide, US): pullback ≤25% is the standard HTF; 25–50% gets reclassified as
  a separate "rocket base" (not disqualified, just a different, looser pattern needing
  6–10 weeks instead of 3–5). No floor is stated — a 5% pullback is fine.
- **IN** (India): "correct sideways no more than 10 to 25%" — states a **band**, implying a
  pullback under ~10% is treated differently (not explicitly ruled invalid, just outside the
  stated range).
- **SH** (US, quant): pullback must be ≤2× the stock's own 20-day ADR% *in addition to* the
  20–25% absolute cap — "less than two times its 20-day ADR percentage." This is the only
  source that normalizes depth by the stock's own volatility rather than using a flat %.
- Net: three different depth philosophies — flat ceiling only (Soreide), a stated band
  (India), and a volatility-normalized cap (SH) — not one number.

**Disagreement #3 — stop-loss placement.**
- **CC / TL1 / CM** (Soreide, all three talks): a fixed **~5%** stop ("I prefer to have a
  stop around five percent," CM: "five percent or less"), stretched to "five to seven
  percent is the most I'll do" (CC) only when a wider stop is unavoidable.
- **IN** (India): stop = **the low of the breakout candle itself** — "your stop loss can be
  kept at the low of the day" — not a fixed percentage; could be much tighter or much wider
  than 5% depending on that day's range.
- **SH** (US, quant): stop-loss should be **a third to a quarter of the 20-day ADR%** — e.g.
  on an 11.07% ADR stock, targeting ~3% risk (a quarter of ADR), explicitly tied to the
  stock's own volatility, not a flat percentage or the raw low-of-day.
- Net: three genuinely different stop rules (fixed %, structural low-of-day, ADR-relative)
  — no single number describes "the" HTF stop across sources.

**Other definitional details (not disputed):**
- Low tight flag: "hopefully like 15% or less in the flag" (**TL1**) — a tighter, less
  common variant.
- Rocket base (failed/slow HTF): pullback 25–50%, needs 6–10 weeks (**TL1**, **CC**).
  Deeper than ~50% pullback in the pole itself: **CC** — "may want to avoid the pattern...
  it could be faulty," reinforced by the FUBO example (61% base decline, "no buy point,"
  **CC**).
- Diagonal trendline confluence: only counts as meaningful with more than two touch points
  (**CC**, **TL1**).
- 10-day EMA discipline (**SH** only): "the creme de la creme... will not close underneath
  their 10-day EMA" during the flag — a stock that violates this is treated as lower
  quality regardless of the pole/flag percentages.
- Price/liquidity floor: don't trade under $5, prefer $10+ (**TL1**, **CC**); "the average
  high tight flag according to stats is about 12.6" (**CC**); position size capped at "no
  more than five percent of the float that trades" (**CC**).
- RS-rating floor (US MarketSmith-specific): "I probably wouldn't attack a high tight flag
  that was under 92 RS... the 91 is cutoff" (**CC**). *Unverified against manas_os*: whether
  manas_os's own relative-strength proxy has an equivalent cutoff was not checked as part of
  this digest (out of the scope named for this pass).
- Volume: should decline through pole-peak to flag-low, then confirm on breakout —
  "10 times relative volume right at the buy point" is the strong-confirmation case (**CC**).

## 2. India adaptation — what source 4 (IN) changes and why

IN is the only India-specific source and its adaptations matter most:

1. **Universe restriction to micro-caps.** IN explicitly rules out large/mid-caps: "our
   Indian stock markets due to participation is comparatively less... all stocks can't move
   120% in a quarter... only pump and dump stocks will move like that" in large/mid-caps. IN
   selects **Nifty Microcap 250** as the stock universe specifically because the pattern
   needs explosive short-term moves that don't occur in liquid large caps in the Indian
   market's lower-participation environment.
2. **Percentage band, not just a ceiling.** As in Disagreement #2 above, IN states pullback
   "no more than 10 to 25%" — a band, versus the US sources' pure ceiling.
3. **Stop = low of breakout day**, not a fixed 5% (Disagreement #3 above) — IN's stated
   reasoning: "if your stock selection is correct, the stock will start performing right
   from day one," so a deep stop isn't needed; a shallow structural stop is used instead.
4. **Realistic target discipline, stated as a % range** (not present as a hard number in any
   US source): "your target is around 30 to 40% or 20 to 30%... book 50% over here when the
   stock makes 20 to 30% and then trail the rest."
5. **"Be selective" heuristic, given as a concrete comparison**: a flag forming *above* the
   stock's all-time high is rated stronger than one forming below it, because "once the
   stock reaches [an old high] it may face a resistance and slowdown" — this is IN's own
   worked comparison (two named example charts, "C2C" vs "Kishi wall" in the transcript)
   applied to relative pattern quality, not a general US rule.
6. **Regime gate is explicit and portfolio-based** — see §4.
7. **Scanning is manual-first**: "manual scanning is the number one scanning method because
   it increases your win rate" (IN's stated belief, not backed by a number in the source —
   flagged in §8 NOT MECHANIZABLE), backstopped by MarketSmith India's preset "potential high
   tide flag" idea-list screen or a free Trendlyne top-gainers/quarter scan that needs manual
   filtering because "it gives most of the stocks that it gives are circuit stocks."
8. **Cupid named as a live example.** IN explicitly names "Cupid" as a stock the flag
   pattern applies to at time of recording ("This is Cupid. The trend is weakening... may
   form a 3 to 5 weeks of consolidation") — see §5, this is independently corroborated by
   our own scan.

## 3. Execution — entry, stop, scaling out, exit

**Entry:**
- Buy only on the close-confirmed breakout above the consolidation zone, never on
  anticipation of one (**IN**: "avoid preempting the trade... enter only after breakout").
- **CC/TL1** (Soreide): buy through the prior day's high or a horizontal pivot; don't chase
  more than "one or two percent past the buy point;" gaps of "two to three percent" on
  earnings are usually avoided unless earnings are an outright beat-and-raise.
- **SH**: intraday entry technique is a 5-minute opening-range breakout (ORB) combined with
  an "IML" (intraday mean-reversion long) near the 10-day EMA, gated on MACD positive +
  intraday relative strength vs. the stock's group + a bullish 5-minute candle with volume.
- **IN**: simplest version — enter on the daily close above the consolidation high; "you can
  even go to daily time frame for better entry" from a weekly-chart-identified setup.
- Buy the full position in one shot ~90% of the time (**CC**): "I'd say 90 percent of the
  time buying all at once," to avoid raising cost basis with add-ons.

**Stop:** see Disagreement #3 above (fixed ~5% Soreide / low-of-day IN / ADR-relative SH).

**Scaling out / risk multiples:**
- Van Tharp risk-multiple framework (**TL1**, **CC**): scale out in units of the initial
  risk taken, not a fixed %. On a strong ("green") signal, hold for "more than two risk
  multiples before you take anything;" in a "total chopfest," take a quick ~20% scale and
  move the stop to breakeven (**TL1**).
- **IN**'s version is a flat %, not risk-multiples: book 50% of the position at 20–30% gain,
  trail the remainder (see §1 Disagreement discussion — this is a genuinely different scaling
  philosophy from Soreide's risk-multiple method, not the same rule in different units).
- Selling into strength generally, keeping a runner: "I'm selling in the strength when I can
  and saving a piece" (**TL1**).

**Exit / stop management after entry:**
- Move stop to breakeven after a defined number of risk multiples once in meaningful profit
  (**CC**, **TL1**); "when you get up 15, 20 [percent]... turn that into a loser [is] a
  stupid move" (**CC**).
- Adjust stops **at the close only**, not intraday: "I'm adjusting it at the close and I'm
  not some kind of random trader... taking it on and off all day" (**CM**) — CM calls
  intraday stop-fiddling "probably the biggest mistake" traders make and don't admit.
- Trail using closes below key daily moving averages (10/21/50-day) once trending (**SH**,
  **CC**, **CM**).

## 4. When it works / when it does NOT — the regime condition

- **IN, stated flatly**: "this pattern only works in the raging bull market. It doesn't work
  in bear market or when the stock market is in sideways move." IN's own gauge for "raging
  bull": not the index, but your own portfolio — "your equity portfolio will increase 1 to 2
  percent every day... that is the time you should understand that we are in a raging bull
  market."
- **CM** (Soreide, in an actual bear/choppy market): "high tight flags aren't working great
  and they don't work great most of the time" in that environment; when they do work, gains
  are capped — "we're looking for five to ten percent and that's probably all we're getting,
  and then you get thrown out on the rest." Soreide's adaptation in that regime is to
  abandon HTF for pullback buys and earnings pivots on established leaders instead.
- **TL1** (bear-market-low case studies): the pattern still appears near the exact bottom of
  a bear market but is unreliable as a textbook setup — "it's not always the case... the
  look is probably most important" (flagged §8, not mechanizable) — success there came from
  identifying market leaders first, pattern second: "getting into the leaders is more
  important than patterns."
- **manas_os's own prior verdict (`design/agents/LENS_HTF.md`, read for this digest)**: the
  system's existing India evidence-tier research (citing `design/Feedback/manas 2.0 - gpt
  2.0.md`) already ranks "high-tight flags / high-ADR discretionary momentum" as **evidence
  tier 7 of 7 — the weakest formal evidence** of all surveyed setup families, describing it
  as "most operator-prone and regime-dependent," concentrated in the "low-float/surveillance
  tier where the tight stop is least reliable," and recommends sizing HTF/flag trades at
  "half or quarter size relative to PEAD or liquid-stock reversal baskets." This is a
  pre-existing internal verdict, not new to this digest — it independently agrees with IN's
  regime-gating caution from a different angle (formal evidence grading vs. anecdotal
  regime observation).

## 6. The choppy-market playbook (source CM)

- **Pullback buying over breakout chasing**: in a choppy/bear tape, CM's stated adaptation
  is "trying to buy leaders on pullbacks" rather than fresh HTF breakouts, specifically
  stocks "that have huge earnings and then pull back because of the market."
- **Earnings pivot vs. earnings gap** — an explicit distinction: "I don't trade earnings
  gaps, I trade what I call earnings pivots." An earnings *gap* is the raw post-earnings
  jump; an earnings *pivot* is a subsequent pullback-and-reclaim of a short-term resistance
  level inside a proper base, entered after the gap has been digested, not on the gap day.
- **Adjust at the close, not intraday**: "you got to adjust it to close" — stops and
  decisions are reviewed once per day at the close; intraday flip-flopping ("chopping
  yourself up all day") is named as the most common unadmitted mistake.
- **Weekly watchlist follow-through test**: build a broad weekly watchlist (CM: "I'll go up
  to maybe 40 on the week"), narrow to a daily focus list ("just like eight or less for the
  day"), then check literally whether anything on the list actually broke out that week —
  "has anything broken out this week, and if everything on the right side just looks weak
  and it's rolling over... you really have to stop yourself from getting too involved" (the
  "looks weak" judgment itself is not numerically defined — flagged in §8).
- **Stage-transition confirmation (Weinstein stage 1→2)**: uses sales-growth acceleration as
  the concrete test, not the chart alone — CM's Crocs example: sales roughly doubled
  year-over-year with accompanying EPS growth as the marker that a name is transitioning out
  of stage 1, not merely basing.
- **Position sizing tied to the red/yellow/green signal**: red signal = "25% or less
  invested," with 5–10% typical individual position size (**CM**); this is the same
  signal-based sizing framework as **TL1**/**CC**, just restated for a choppy tape.

## 7. Already in manas_os / not yet in manas_os

Verified by reading `manas_os/scanner/ep_quality.py` and `manas_os/scanner/discovery_metrics.py`
directly (2026-07-26).

**Already implemented:**
- `discovery_metrics.ema10_respect` (lines ~249-295) — directly implements the **SH** 10-day
  EMA discipline rule from this exact transcript set. Its own docstring already cites it:
  *"From the 2026-07-26 high-tight-flag transcripts: 'the creme de la creme will find
  support, hold around, shake out UNDER their 10-day EMA.'"* It returns `respect_pct` (% of
  last 60 sessions closing above 10EMA) and `shakeout_holds` (sessions whose low pierced the
  10EMA but whose close held above it — explicitly scored as a positive signal, not a
  violation, matching **SH**'s "shake out off the 10day" framing). **Shadow-only**: no gate,
  no rank yet.
- `discovery_metrics.adr20` — the 20-day ADR% used throughout **SH**'s stop-sizing and
  depth-normalization rules already exists as a scoreable metric (not display-only).
- `discovery_metrics.leg_linearity` / `base_symmetry` — measure the "smooth 45-degree pole,
  no base inside it" and "two-sided contracting flag" qualities that **CC** describes
  narratively ("smooth rise," "you don't want a base in the pole") but never gives a formal
  metric for; these two functions are the closest existing quantification, though they were
  built for a different (user's own current) doctrine, not derived from these HTF
  transcripts specifically.
- `discovery_metrics.up_day_persistence` — implements the Stockbee `CountTrue(c>c1,252)`
  contrast used in §5's worked example.
- `ep_quality.py`'s `BURST_GAP_PCT` (5.0%) / `BURST_GAP_RVOL` (2.0×) vs. `fresh_base_breakout`
  split, checked directly: these are **not** an HTF pole/flag detector. `burst_nature()`
  classifies whether a stock's *own historical burst days* (single-day gaps ≥5% on ≥2×
  volume, or a ≥8% one-day change) tend to hold or fade over the next 5–10 sessions.
  `location_read()`'s `fresh_base_breakout` (breakout age ≤3 bars, own-history tightness
  percentile ≤25) and its extension test (close > 1.08× EMA21) are reusable building blocks
  for a future HTF detector (tightness, extension, room-above-resistance) but the module
  answers a different question — "is this specific burst tradeable as a swing" — not "is
  this a 100%-pole-then-tight-flag."
  - **But one HTF-adjacent rule IS gated there, and it is dead.** `classify()` awards
    `SWING_EP` only when `fresh_base_breakout AND room_above AND not extended AND nature in
    {swing, mixed}` — so a bare gap can never earn SWING_EP; it degrades to `INTRADAY_SIP`.
    That is exactly **CM**'s earnings-*pivot*-not-earnings-*gap* rule, already encoded as a
    hard requirement. It has never fired: `FRESH_BASE_BREAKOUT_AGE_MAX = 3` while the measured
    median `breakout_age` across the universe is **172**, so `fresh_base_breakout` is
    effectively always False and `SWING_EP` is unreachable. Fixing the pivot semantics is
    therefore the single blocker on the module's "FIX then wire" verdict — not new detection
    logic.

**Not yet in manas_os:**
- No function anywhere in `scanner/discovery_metrics.py` (or elsewhere found by search)
  computes the HTF-specific triad directly: pole % over a rolling window, flag depth % of
  that pole, and flag length in bars, as one named metric. The §5 "measured on our universe"
  scan (pole ≥60% in ≤40 bars, flag ≤25% and ≤2.0× ADR20) was run as an ad hoc analysis, not
  as a persisted, reusable scanner function — confirmed by grep across `manas_os/scanner/`
  finding no `flagpole`/`flag_length`/`pole_pct` symbol.
- No IN-style all-time-high-vs-below-all-time-high relative quality comparison (§2 point 5)
  exists as a metric.
- No ADR-relative stop-loss *selection* rule (**SH**'s "a quarter to a third of 20-day ADR%")
  is wired into `risk/plan.py`'s `choose_stop` — that function uses structure-based stop logic,
  not this ADR-relative rule.
  - Partial credit, added 2026-07-26 for accuracy: the stop **cap** in `risk/plan.py` *is* now
    ADR-scaled (`ADR_MULT_BY_REGIME` = 1.5 / 1.25 / 1.0 × ADR by regime, `STOP_ADR_MULT_MAX`
    = 2.0). So the tool already reasons about stops in ADR units — but as a **ceiling**, at
    **1.0–1.5× ADR**, versus SH's **target** of **0.25–0.33× ADR**. That is a 4–6× gap, and it
    is structural rather than a mistake: SH's stop sits under a 5-minute opening-range bar,
    ours under a daily structure level. Since `qty = capital × risk% / (entry − stop)`, the
    same rupee risk buys 4–6× fewer shares. This is the quantified cost of being EOD-only and
    is the strongest argument in these transcripts for finishing the live intraday loop —
    intraday confirmation is what earns the tighter stop, and the tighter stop is what earns
    the size.
- No risk-multiple-based partial-exit ladder (**TL1**/**CC**'s Van Tharp-style scaling) vs.
  IN's flat 20–30%-then-trail rule is implemented or reconciled anywhere found in this pass.
- No RS-rating (or manas_os-equivalent) floor tied specifically to HTF candidates was
  verified — out of scope for this pass (see §1).

## 8. NOT MECHANIZABLE

Vague statements sources make with no stated number — recorded here rather than assigned an
invented threshold:

- **"The look is probably most important"** (**TL1**) — visual/gestalt judgment of chart
  quality, explicitly placed above any single scanned metric.
- **"Spirit of a pivot" / "spirit of one"** (**TL1**, borrowed from a trader named Mike
  Webster) — a deliberately loose, non-textbook pivot read used specifically when "waiting
  for the perfect textbook thing" would mean missing the trade entirely off a bear-market
  low. No numeric substitute is given.
- **"It kind of talks to me over time"** (**CC**) — pattern recognition attributed to
  accumulated screen-time / experience ("seat time"), not a rule.
- **"The tighter they are, the more you can just buy the top"** (**CC**) — a qualitative
  heuristic relating visual tightness to entry aggressiveness, no tightness number given.
- **"Manual scanning... increases your win rate like anything"** (**IN**) — asserted as a
  belief, no win-rate figure or comparison test given.
- **Batting-average self-assessment**: "do you suck right now versus... are things really
  hot right now" (**CC**) — a trader's subjective read of their own current edge used to
  size up or down; no threshold stated.
- **CM's "looks weak and it's rolling over"** in the weekly watchlist follow-through test
  (§6) — the pass/fail judgment on whether the watchlist "confirmed" is visual, not numeric.
- **"You have to be selective"** (**IN**) as a general instruction is mechanizable in its one
  worked comparison (all-time-high context, §2), but as a general instruction on its own
  carries no threshold and should not be treated as one.
