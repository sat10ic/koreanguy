## VCP (Volatility Contraction Pattern) — base types
Definition (lines 17-31): successive price swings whose RANGE shrinks each time
("रेंज ... गेटिंग स्मॉलर एंड स्मॉलर, स्कवीज हो रहा है"). Mechanism narrated as an
institution defending its cost basis: each dip fails to reach the prior low because a
large buyer keeps absorbing supply above it, until supply is exhausted and a single
volume pump completes the breakout (lines 21-31).
**Three quality tiers, by name and exact ranking** (lines 33-43):
1. **Strongest** — previous low is defended AND the selling above is itself tilting
   downward (sellers giving up ground too).
2. **Middle** — previous low is defended, sellers hold roughly flat above (no tilt
   either way). "मैं अंदर बेस के अंदर क्या हो रहा है वो ज्यादा इंपोर्टेंट होता है" — what
   happens INSIDE the base matters more than which of tiers 1/2 it is.
3. **Weakest** — lower highs with flat lows ("लोअर हाई एंड फ्लैट लोज"). Speaker's own
   scoring logic (lines 35-43): flat-high = 0 pts, higher-low = +1; net "weak" reading =
   lower-high (-1) canceling a higher-low (+1) → net 0, still weak; the worst case is
   lower-high AND lower-low together = clearly weakest, must weigh candles/volume/sector
   context alongside, never VCP shape alone.

## Full pattern catalogue (speaker's own count: SIX)
QUOTE (line 51-53): **"आई हैव डिड्यूस की छ पैटर्न होते हैं"** = (1) VCP/TVCP (3
sub-variants above), (2) Inverted-Head-&-Shoulders, (3) IHS-on-trendline, (4) Crow bar,
(5) Hook, (6) Fast flag. Definitions (lines 59-71):
- **Crow bar**: one big candle, then 2-3 SMALL candles that do NOT retrace back into the
  big candle's range, then continuation. "EMA tries to catch up to price, but can't."
- **Hook**: price DOES retest back down to the EMA/VWAP zone before continuing ("प्राइस
  ईए की तरफ आता है").
- **Fast flag**: sideways for "मोर देन 4 डेज" without the candles coming back down — "EMA
  catches up to price," not the reverse.
These 3 work across intraday/daily/weekly timeframes and even inside IPO bases (lines
69-73): if the IPO-day candle itself already looks like a completed move, subsequent
tightness = crow bar; if it opens lower first, treat as a normal base instead.

## IPO-specific candle/context rules
- IPO bases are explicitly called **"पैटर्न लेट सेटअप"** — pattern-led, not
  volume-led — precisely BECAUSE the base is small: "जितना छोटा रहेगा बेस उतना कैंडल
  स्टिक्स की इंपॉर्टेंस ज्यादा हो जाती है" (lines 47-51). Volume is explicitly downgraded:
  "वॉल्यूम इतना मैटर नहीं करता" for small IPO bases (line 51, restated 195-197: "वॉल्यूम
  देखने की जरूरत ही नहीं है ... कैंडल का साइज ज्यादा इंपॉर्टेंट है").
- **Age-based rule graduation** (lines 263-267, 453-469): base < ~2 weeks = pure
  pattern+candle play; as base grows toward ~2 months, volume's importance rises and
  candle importance falls; beyond ~2 months, "बड़े बेस" rules apply (symmetry,
  cleanliness, liquidity) — explicitly **out of scope for this video** ("हम ये कवर नहीं
  कर रहे").
- Long-tail / bottom-spring candles (lines 75-81): long lower wick = demand ("शेक आउट");
  "bottom spring" = a down-day fully engulfed by the next up-day = strong positive.
- **50%-retracement rule** (lines 99-105, restated 197-199, 705-711): the strong IPO-day
  candle's range should NOT retrace >50% on a later down day — "अगर कोई कैंडल पॉजिटिव
  क्लोज कर रहा है इट इज अ वेरी ... पॉजिटिव साइन," and conversely a violently red first
  candle inside a base is flagged as bad price action.
- **Shake-out-during-market-fall rule** (lines 99-101): a single ugly candle that
  coincides with a broad market sell-off (worked example: 12-13 Nov market fall) with NO
  follow-through down move = a shake-out, not a breakdown — many traders wrongly drop the
  name here.
- BSE→NSE relistings behave exactly like fresh IPOs and should be traded the same way
  (lines 171-175): "उसका पूरा बिहेवियर आईपीओ वाला ही होता है."

## Entry triggers — multi-timeframe (MTF)
Timeframe stack, importance order (lines 81-85): **Daily > 75-min > 15-min**; "15-75-daily
आर मोर देन एनफ" for most people — going below 15-min ("1-min") is possible but catches
very few extra setups and multiplies trade frequency/errors. "First inside bar" after
listing is called out repeatedly as the single best trigger (lines 87-89, 129-131,
205-207, 417-419: "जभी भी ना फर्स्ट इनसाइड बार होता है इट इज अ गुड ट्रिगर").
Worked SL-compression example (lines 89-95): daily-timeframe SL = 3.4%; dropping to
75-min compresses it toward ~1.4-2%; general target stated: **"ट्राय करो कि 4% ज्यादा
स्टॉप लॉस ना हो"** for freshly-listed IPOs — "मैथमेटिकली इट्स नॉट गुड" beyond that.

## Sizing / trailing
Trailing style is discretionary, price-action- and market-condition-dependent (lines
107-109): default holds to at least ~20% before trimming; sometimes to 40% if price
action looks strong; typical split = sell half around 15-20%, trail the rest via 10 or
20 EMA per personal conviction. No fixed universal number given beyond the "at least 20%"
floor.

## Avoid-list (explicit, this transcript)
- Setups needing "इतना दिमाग" (excessive interpretive effort) to justify — line 201-203
  example (a stock the speaker admits he likely would NOT trade in real time despite it
  working in hindsight): "मैं पर्सनली शायद इसको ट्रेड नहीं लेता."
- Very illiquid names even with a clean pattern — several explicit call-outs (e.g. Ravi
  Money area ~185, 245) where liquidity alone kills an otherwise valid setup.
- Entering "too far into the move" — once a stock is already 100%+ retraced/extended off
  its reaction, the IPO/EP "extra point" no longer applies (cross-referenced from Part 3,
  lines 405-409) — treat as a normal base on its own thrust/linearity merits only.
- Multi-hit resistance / "hard to trade" bases with poor candle quality (violent
  rejection wicks, big red bodies late in the base) — repeatedly flagged as lower
  win-rate even when they occasionally work (e.g. lines 415-435, 443-451 case-by-case).
