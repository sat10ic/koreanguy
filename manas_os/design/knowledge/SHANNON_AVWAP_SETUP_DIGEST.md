## 1. THE setup: "Chase the gap or wait for VWAP"
Named by him exactly that (line 137: "this is what I call chase the gap or wait for VWAP
is what we're building into a strategy"). Structure (lines 133-141):
1. Stock gaps up on an event (earnings, upgrade, hot group — cause optional).
2. It runs a few minutes, then drops BELOW the day's VWAP (gap-chasers now under water,
   shorts pressing).
3. It levels out and **crosses back above the daily VWAP** — that reclaim is the entry:
   "it gets back above that daily [average] price 22 minutes into the day. So that's
   where I love to buy gaps."
4. **Stop: underneath the low of the day.** His stated odds — flagged VERBATIM because he
   immediately disclaims them: "And 60% of the time it won't violate that. 40% of the
   time it will... **And I'm making those numbers up**, but I'm saying the majority of the
   time it does continue" (line 141). → the 60/40 is explicitly invented on the spot;
   treat as "majority," never as a measured statistic.
5. Context filter: works best "when you have a clean chart... with a stage two uptrend on
   the bigger time frame" (line 141).
Trade management (lines 145-157):
- No price targets ("I don't really do price targets," line 403). Daily pivot R2/R3 are
  "levels of interest" — when tagged, switch to **two-minute bars**: hold the first third
  while each 2-min bar makes a higher low; sell the first third the moment a 2-min bar
  breaks the prior 2-min low ("the two-minute exit"). Rationale: he enters A+ trades at
  MAXIMUM size because entry precision is his strength, so the first-third sale takes him
  back to normal size (lines 299-301).
- Re-buy the sold third on the next VWAP bounce ("undercut the VWAP by 10 cents, gets back
  above, I buy at 15 cents higher. My stop is 15 cents on that," line 151-153 — worked
  example numbers, not universal constants).
- If it closes strong on a genuine earnings gap: hold a portion against the EVENT anchor
  for weeks — "sometimes it will hold above it for the next month and ideally I'm going to
  hold at least a portion of that for that month" (line 155).
- Swing exit = EITHER the event-anchor break (see §3) OR trend-definition break: a lower
  low on ~30-minute bars against the successively higher pullback lows of the last ~15
  days (line 157). Explicitly fractal — same logic scales to daily/weekly (line 159).

## 2. Anchor selection rules
His own three-category taxonomy, stated as such (lines 209-213):
1. **Event anchors** — earnings ("just fantastic because it combines the fundamentals
   along with the technicals and who's in control," line 209); Federal Reserve
   announcements ("often be good for three to eight to 10 days," line 209 — vague range,
   only matters when the Fed move was unexpected); by extension news/catalyst gaps.
2. **Time-based anchors** — daily VWAP, week-to-date, month-to-date, year-to-date
   (line 211). YTD is his workhorse companion anchor (Fastly and SPY examples).
3. **Price anchors** — significant highs and lows (line 211-213): the year's low (April
   low), the year-to-date high, intraday swing highs ("it runs 10% in the morning, hits a
   high, pulls back 3%. Well, put an anchor on that high," line 205).
Additional anchor sources appearing in worked examples:
- **IPO/listing day**: he tracked SanDisk "with the anchor from the IPO" (line 369);
  first-volume-spike importance noted at line 437.
- **Momentum-campaign start**: "That was a new momentum campaign right there" (line 215)
  — the ignition bar of a fresh advance becomes an anchor.
- **Handoff / re-anchoring rule** (his explicit answer to "when do you re-anchor"): when
  an old anchor is far away and untested, anchor NEXT from **the last place the prior
  anchor was touched** — "our next level of interest to anchor from was the last place
  that anchor was touched" (line 221); interviewer names these "handoffs" and Shannon
  confirms: "these handoff levels where it touches each one of these and then from the
  beginning of a new momentum campaign" (line 227). Old anchors are dormant, not dead:
  "that anchor is down at 686. There's no value in that to us right now, but it doesn't
  mean there won't be in the future" (line 215).
- **Choosing between anchors**: he doesn't pick one — he stacks them and treats AGREEMENT
  as the signal (see confluence, §3). No numeric priority weights are stated anywhere.
Calculation method (lines 437-443, Certain, direct answer to a viewer question): typical
price = **OHLC/4, always** ("it's open and high low close divided by four always for me");
tick data is the only "true" VWAP; granularity ladder for intraday anchoring — 1-min chart
for the first 10 minutes, 2-min after 10 minutes, 5-min after 30 minutes (1-min all day
for extreme ~28%-ATR movers).

## 3. Intervention areas / signals (each with his framing)
1. **Daily-VWAP reclaim after a gap** — the §1 entry.
2. **Event-anchor loss = exit cue** — "if it breaks below it, that's my cue to get out
   because now the average person over that month since the event is now in a losing
   position [and] the sellers have the opportunity to take control" (line 155-157).
3. **First test of an earnings-gap anchor = buy the BOUNCE, never the touch** — "If it
   holds in here, I'm going to look to buy the bounce from that, but I'm not going to buy
   the touch. There's a big difference" (line 163). Restated as doctrine: "I don't buy
   the pullback to the VWAP because it might hold or it might just slice right through
   it. It's not for me to decide... let other people [do] the dirty work" (line 451-453).
   Confirmation = back above the 5-day MA, a higher high, and a definable higher low to
   risk against (lines 175-177).
4. **Confluence of anchors = level of interest, never "support"** — Fastly worked
   example: earnings anchor + year-to-date anchor + 50-day MA in one zone; each cohort
   defends its own reference, sideline cash bids, shorts cover (lines 165-173). His
   epistemology, verbatim: "we only know support and resistance in anything in hindsight"
   (line 259) and "they're levels of interest... when more people come to the same
   conclusion in that same area it kind of increases the odds" (lines 259-261).
5. **AVWAP-from-highs = overhead resistance; reclaiming it = buyers back in control** —
   downtrend rallies fail at the anchor-from-high; the gap up through it marked "where
   the buyers were back in control" (lines 227-229, SPY). Interviewer frames the
   breakout-trader application (early right-hand-side entries often ARE the break through
   the anchor-from-highs) and Shannon works the example rather than disputing it
   (lines 237-243).
6. **AVWAP pinch** — price squeezed between a falling anchor-from-high and a rising
   anchor-from-low. He half-regrets coining it: "I kind of wish I never wrote about them...
   technically, this is a pinch, but it's a terrible pinch because it's so wide. What I
   want to see in a pinch is... it really compresses... like what [Minervini] calls a
   volatility contraction pattern" (lines 329-333). A valid pinch therefore requires
   genuine tightness at the apex; buying a wide pinch after an 8% run-up is explicitly
   rejected (USFD losing-trade case, lines 327-335).
7. **Multi-day VWAP stack** — today's VWAP above the 2-day, 3-day, 4-day, 5-day VWAPs,
   above a rising 5-day SMA = permission context for the gap play (line 401, IWM).
8. **Daily floor-trader pivots as secondary levels** — "the average stock on a given day
   will trade between daily S2 and daily R2 87% of the time" (line 403, cited as "a study
   I saw once"; NOTE: he says "Only 13%" outside the range at line 403 and "only 17%"
   two sentences later — internally inconsistent in-source; treat the exact figure as
   soft). Used to trigger the two-minute exit, not as targets.
9. **5-day moving average regime filter** — rising 5-day = "innocent until proven guilty"
   (shakeout dips tolerated); declining 5-day = "guilty till proven innocent" (line
   427-431). Definition is precise and quotable: the true rolling week = 390 minutes/day
   × 5 = 1,950 minutes → 195-period MA on 10-min bars, 130-period on 15-min (lines
   421-425).
10. **Stop mechanics** (lines 307-317): stops 2 (max 3) cents below the governing higher
    low; if conditions are "squirrely," split the stop half at the near low and half at a
    deeper prior low; **never leave a stop working into the open** — give the stock the
    first 5 minutes, then as "minute 6 begins" place the stop 2 cents under the
    first-5-minutes recovery low. Trend-death exit: a higher high followed by a break of
    the prior higher low = "trend is over. Got to get out" (line 309).
11. **Sizing** = R-multiple: fixed currency risk per trade; share count scales inversely
    with stop distance (his $100k/$1,000/25-cent worked example, lines 305-309), with a
    self-check against manufacturing artificially tight stops just to buy more shares.

## 5. DELTA vs `avwap_auto_anchor` (manas_os/engine/eod_detectors.py:524-637)
What the code does: over a 120-bar window it collects candidates — "earnings-gap"
(gap ≥4% AND vol >1.5× avg20; significance 3), "breakout" (close > 20-day high AND vol
>1.5×; sig 2), "swing-low" (strict 9-bar low; sig 1), +1 sig if vol >2× — keeps the
newest candidate per type, picks the single highest-(significance, recency), computes one
AVWAP series from it using **close×volume**, and holds the prior anchor by default unless
the challenger is newer, strictly more significant, the prior has aged ≥15 bars, and the
two are >5 bars apart.
- **MATCHES**: (a) event-first ranking — earnings-gap outranking breakout outranking
  swing-low agrees with his event > price-anchor emphasis; (b) volume confirmation on
  anchor days mirrors his "biggest volume ever" anchoring instinct (SanDisk, line 365);
  (c) the keep-prior-anchor stability default is directionally faithful to his "old
  anchors stay relevant until events supersede" stance; (d) a swing-low anchor type is a
  legitimate member of his price-anchor category.
- **DIFFERS — single anchor vs. confluence (the biggest delta)**: the function returns
  exactly ONE anchor/series. Shannon's entire framework is MULTI-anchor: the signal IS the
  agreement of the earnings anchor + YTD anchor + (say) 50-day MA in one zone, plus a
  separate anchor-from-highs acting as resistance. A one-anchor engine structurally cannot
  represent a confluence zone or a pinch.
- **DIFFERS — no anchors-from-HIGHS**: all three code anchor types fire on strength/lows.
  There is no 52-week-high / swing-high / year-high anchor, so resistance AVWAPs,
  reclaim-of-anchor-from-high signals, and pinches are unrepresentable.
- **DIFFERS — no time-based anchors**: no year-to-date / month-to-date anchor types
  (his second category, and half of every confluence example he gave).
- **DIFFERS — typical price**: code accumulates `close*volume`; his stated method is
  OHLC/4 (line 441). On daily bars this is a real, quantifiable series difference (wide-
  range days pull OHLC/4 away from close), and it is directly fixable.
- **DIFFERS — re-anchor logic**: code re-anchors on an aging heuristic (≥15 bars + higher
  significance). His rules are structural: re-anchor at a NEW momentum campaign, or hand
  off to the last-touch point of the prior anchor. No "last touch" concept exists in code.
- **DIFFERS — "earnings-gap" naming**: the code's sig-3 anchor is any ≥4%/1.5× gap; it
  never checks an earnings calendar. He treats earnings as the premier anchor precisely
  BECAUSE of the fundamental event; a catalyst-blind gap is a weaker citation. (The 4%
  and 1.5× numbers themselves are code inventions — this transcript quantifies no gap or
  volume threshold for anchor selection.)
- **MISSING — signal layer**: the function outputs a series only. Reclaim/loss/
  first-test-bounce/pinch/stack — everything in §3 — is unimplemented downstream of it
  (unverified beyond this file's own callers; flagged rather than asserted for the wider
  codebase).
- **MISSING — IPO anchor type**: `listing` data exists in this same file (`ipo_base`),
  but `avwap_auto_anchor` takes no listing input and has no listing-day anchor.

## 6. ENGINEERING SPEC — deterministic multi-anchor engine (faithful to his rules)
Scope: watchlist + focus-list + open-position symbols only. **Anchor selection is
rules-based from the inventory below — never model-guessed.** Daily OHLCV inputs; typical
price = (O+H+L+C)/4 (Certain, his stated method).
**Anchor inventory per symbol** (each anchor = {date, type, source_rule}):
- `earnings`: each earnings date (from the earnings calendar; fall back to
  gap+volume-confirmed days labeled `event_gap` when no calendar row — keep the two types
  distinct so provenance stays honest). [Certain — his #1 anchor]
- `ytd`: first trading day of the calendar year. `mtd` optional/display-only. [Certain]
- `ipo`: listing day (reuse the `listing` dict). [Certain — SanDisk example]
- `high_52w` and `year_high`: the bar of the trailing-252 high / YTD high →
  anchors-from-highs (resistance role). [Certain — SPY example]
- `major_low`: the bar of the trailing-252 low or a confirmed major swing low. [Certain]
- `campaign`: ignition bar of a new momentum campaign — deterministic proxy: breakout
  above N-day high on ≥K× volume (N=20/K=1.5 may be REUSED from the existing detector but
  must be marked Assumption: Shannon quantifies neither). [Likely shape / Assumption nums]
- `handoff`: when price first TOUCHES an active anchor (within tolerance ε) and holds,
  register that touch bar as a new candidate anchor. [Certain concept; ε is Assumption —
  he gives no tolerance]
**Anchor lifecycle**: anchors never get deleted — they go DORMANT when >D% away from price
(display/score weight 0) and reactivate on approach ("no value... right now, but it
doesn't mean there won't be," line 215). D is an Assumption (unquantified in-source).
**Anchor respect scoring** (his description is qualitative only — Likely): increment a
respect counter each time price approaches within ε and bounces without a close beyond;
more historical touches ⇒ higher level-of-interest weight ("price memory... the more
volume that traded there, the more likely," line 263). Weights are Assumption.
**Signal set** (deterministic, per §3): `gap_vwap_reclaim` (§1 entry, EOD approximation:
close back above day-anchored AVWAP after an intraday loss — true form needs intraday
data); `event_anchor_loss` (close below an event anchor held since the event — exit cue);
`first_test_bounce` (first return to an earnings anchor: flag the ZONE, confirm only on
bounce + higher high — never signal the touch itself); `confluence` (count of active
anchors + 20/50-day MAs within ε of each other near price; report the count, not a
buy signal); `avwap_from_high_reclaim` (close above an anchor-from-highs = control flip);
`pinch` (falling anchor-from-high and rising anchor-from-low converging AND apex band
< tightness threshold T of price — T unquantified in-source, Assumption; a wide pinch
must NOT fire, per his USFD self-criticism); `vwap_stack` (multi-window VWAPs ordered
ascending under price — context flag). Every signal carries {anchor_dates, rule_cite}.
