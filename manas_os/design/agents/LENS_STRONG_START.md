# LENS: Strong Start (Manas Arora's Entry Process)

Source coverage: **STRONG**. Primary source is Manas Arora's own course chapter on entries
(`Course Notes/6 Manas Entry.md`), cross-checked against the Strong Start Tightness Study
(`Course Notes/cleaned/Manas/Strong_Start_Tightness_Study.md`) and the study digest
(`design/study/Manas Arora/main.md`).

You are debating whether a candidate qualifies as a **Strong Start** (or its sibling,
**Busted**) setup in Arora's own process — not a generic breakout system. Use his vocabulary.

## 1. RECOGNITION markers (price/volume/chart, concrete)

**Strong Start (gap continuation), from `6 Manas Entry.md`:**
- Stock closed at price X yesterday; today's open gaps at or above yesterday's HIGH, or at minimum
  clears the previous close and holds.
- "The low of the next day should not really breach the previous day's close." Minor breaches
  (a few ticks, ~20-30) are acceptable ("reasonable breach"); a clear breach into the prior range
  invalidates the setup.
- Wait **2-3 minutes after the open** (9:15 → 9:17/9:18) before acting — do not buy at 9:15.
- Entry trigger: price crosses **above the previous day's high** after that 2-3 minute
  confirmation window.
- Bonus (not mandatory) signal: **8-10%+ of average daily volume already printed in the first
  2-3 minutes** — "relative volume" (RVOL) indicator. If choosing between two names with an
  equal Strong Start and only one shows this early RVOL, take the one with volume.
- Gap must not be excessive: "avoid it if the gap is already some 5-6%" — a too-large gap wrecks
  risk-reward because the stop gets pushed too wide.

**Busted (15-minute reversal trigger), same source:**
- Applies within an already-qualified daily setup (reversal or pullback) — "the stock on the
  daily frame has to be a great setup" first; Busted is only the entry mechanics on a lower (15-min)
  timeframe.
- Identify an important reaction/support level on the smaller frame (a prior swing low, or the
  low of a high-volume/purple-dot bar).
- Place **alert #1** at that level. When breached, place **alert #2** above the high of the
  breach bar.
- Alert #2 must trigger **within 2-3 bars on the 15-min chart (30-45 minutes max)** — "sooner
  the better." If it takes longer, the setup is disqualified: "the quality of that reversal
  really goes down."
- On trigger: buy at/near the high of the breach bar; stop goes below the breached low.
- Can be repeated to build multiple positions on the same name as new reaction points form
  (Zentech example: up to 8-9 entries across weeks).

**Strong Start Tightness precondition** (from the Tightness Study — empirical, not theoretical):
- ">80% of good Strong Start results had an extremely tight previous day" — small candle/narrow
  range vs. the stock's own recent bars, i.e. volatility contraction right before the Strong Start.
  The same discipline applies to **shakeout** entries generally: a shakeout alone is not enough —
  require tightness *plus* the shakeout; repeated loose bars into the shakeout are a reason to
  pass rather than take it as a valid reversal trigger. [W15, avg stocks.txt ~31%]
- No fixed numeric threshold given — Arora explicitly declines to quantify it: "memorize the
  pictures... compare the size of the current day with the previous days of the same stock."
- Exception: a stock down ~5 consecutive days does NOT need the tight-day precondition — that's
  a different (reversal) structure, not the tightness pattern.

## 2. CONTEXT requirements (regime/sector/liquidity)

- The daily-frame setup must be sound BEFORE any Strong Start/Busted entry is considered — two
  base setups feed it: **Continuation** (uptrend → pullback to rising 20-DMA → tight clean
  consolidation) or **Reversal** (uptrend → 3-5 red days, low volume on the down days, buying
  force intact) — per `main.md` Chapter 5/6 digest.
- Buying force / prior strength check cited repeatedly in the entry chapter's own examples:
  Parag Milk was "58% up from the lowest point in three months" before its Strong Start;
  BSOFT was "31% up" before its reversal Strong Start.
- Weekly cross-check (per digest of Ch.5): reject if weekly is extended (>20% from 10-week MA,
  3+ green weeks running, 3+ bases old).
- Market environment gates aggressiveness overall (main.md Ch.4): only go aggressive on Strong
  Starts when signs favor it (breadth extreme low, index refusing to make new lows, several
  bases forming) — defensive market = trail tighter / fewer new Strong Start entries.

## 3. DISQUALIFIERS

- Low of the day convincingly breaches the previous close (not a "touch and go" — a real break).
- Gap already 5-6%+ at open — risk-reward destroyed, "I will pass on that trade."
- **Buying on the 4th (or later) green day in a row and calling it a Strong Start** — explicitly
  invalidated in the Tightness Study even when it "worked": "why do you want to pocket only the
  remaining two days and enter in the middle of the move?" This is a hard disqualifier regardless
  of outcome — Arora excludes it from his own win sample on principle.
- Busted alert #2 fails to trigger within 2-3 bars / 30-45 min — abandon, don't chase late.
- No daily-frame setup underneath a Busted attempt — never take a 15-min reaction on a
  structurally poor daily chart.
- Previous day not tight (no volatility contraction) AND stock is not in the "down 5 days"
  exception category — lower-quality Strong Start candidate, deprioritize on the watchlist.

## 4. GOOD vs BAD example (in words)

**GOOD** — Parag Milk (16 June entry, from `6 Manas Entry.md`): clean prior downtrend → uptrend →
very tight consolidation, 58% buying force from 3-month low. Next morning: gap-up holding above
prior close, low never touches the previous close, price crosses prior high after the 2-3 min
wait. Entered at ~111 vs. a textbook breakout entry far higher — "definitely making more money
today than somebody who entered here."

**GOOD** — BSOFT reversal Strong Start: stock up 31%, then 5 red days in a row on falling volume
(buying force intact, no institutional selling). Next day: strong start above prior close/high,
maintained through the 2-3 min window → entered near the day's low risk, rode to new highs.

**BAD** — the DLF-like case in the Tightness Study: technically a valid-looking Strong Start,
entered on the 4th green day in the move under the reasoning "this stock usually runs 6-7 days."
Arora rejects this as a process violation even though the trade could work — it trains the habit
of ignoring the move's maturity and entering "in the middle," which eventually gives back the
gains from luckier trades.

**BAD (implicit)** — any candidate gapping 5-6%+ with a wide potential stop, or breaching the
previous close by more than a "touch and go" at the low — described directly as trades to pass on.

## 5. Exit / failure notes

- Not covered in the entry chapter itself (that's a separate "Ride or Sell" chapter — see
  main.md Ch.8 digest): young market/young move → do not interfere, move stop to breakeven after
  first strong day, trail with 20-DMA on a **closing basis**, emergency stop below the latest
  swing low.
- Position sizing on entry: beginners use ~3-4% stop (max), reducing toward day's low (~2-2.5%)
  after 20-30 trades of experience (`6 Manas Entry.md`).
- Busted-specific stop: placed just below the breached reaction level.
- If a Strong Start / Busted trade stops out, Arora's separate note (main.md "mas6") says put it
  on a watch-for-re-entry list — a stopped-out name that sets up again gets priority ("weak
  hands flushed, next move cleaner"), but that is a general Arora rule, not Strong-Start-specific,
  cited here only as a downstream pointer.
