Hi guys, welcome to the new chapter: Becoming Aggressive and Defensive in Trading with Market Index Analysis.

In this chapter we are going to find out the right environment in which our trading style works, and the environment where the odds are not in your favor. Whatever you try, these strategies will not really give out the same results. What I have seen is that people — especially the new ones and the struggling ones — are trading just one way throughout the year. In some months their strategy works and in other months it stops working. The person who made money in good months starts doubting the strategy, and then you jump from one strategy to another.

Now, when you are starting out I would not recommend that you jump, but we can definitely decide our approach: whether we need to be aggressive or defensive. This is what we are going to learn today.

If there are only that many stocks and you're already down — only 15% or so are up, the majority are down — that's almost like a worst-case scenario from here. Historically you can't really go bad from this because everything is down, and in that sort of market you can imagine there will be very little retail participation. The weak hands are out. Only the seasoned players who know that markets will not stay down forever enter in these kinds of markets.

When we hit 1,400–1,500, this is when — I'll show you with a graph — the markets are at a top. The stock market is making headlines on the front page of the newspaper, and that's exactly when the new person reads the news and shows interest in entering the market. Then the seasoned players who bought around the lowest level dump their shares to this new money that has no idea how the market works.

You are okay with this in this kind of environment because the upside is really good and you have to just be patient enough.

## Why Environment Matters More Than the Setup

That was my approach in February and March this year. When you know that — and let me tell you one important thing that a lot of people ignore — just like in the beginning of this video: the entire year you are trading the same way because you are focused on individual setups. You are just focused on, "Okay, the stock is looking really great. There's a cup and handle, there's a tangle breakout," but you are ignoring the environment. That is why there are certain months where your breakout will work really well, the breakouts are there, and when you are in a positive environment, you really feel that "Okay, you have cracked it," and you tweet a lot and you share a lot and you even think of taking leverage and what not. Suddenly when that happens, the market breadth is already around 1,400–1,500, and you don't realize that we have come so far that the upside is limited — but you still continue, because you are ignorant of this fact. You still continue the same way, and suddenly your tangles are not working.

The environment is so important to me. There are certain months — just now, from March to today — we have made a lot of money. A lot of stocks broke out. In fact, if you've been regularly trading, you will notice: in these 3–4 months, all of a sudden so many stocks broke out. They not only broke out well; they went up 40–50%. I have Zen Technologies (ZENTEC) — Corrected (raw: "Zentech") — which went up 100%. So many stocks went up 30–40% in my portfolio. Why? What happened in these 2–3 months?

After these 2–3 months, again, if you've been trading enough, you notice that things are not working out well. Now the stocks are moving up only 5–7%. If you can recognize that environment — the good environment — right before it is about to come, and if you also recognize that the environment is now about to get over or is already over, then you either take smaller bets after that, changing your approach from aggressive to defensive. You take smaller bets, or you take faster gains. You are not sitting on your trades for long. Or the second approach is you completely sit out and decide to trade only when you have your environment with you.

The person who does not make these changes is the one who's always suffering, because the gains made in these 2–3 good months get given back in just 2–3 weeks when the environment changes. Then the frustration sets in. You're demotivated. You're demoralized, and then you think of quitting this business because you put in so much effort making gains in a few months and you end up giving it back in only a few weeks — because you did not align yourself with the environment. You don't even know the right environment in which your trading style works. You just focus on stocks. You just focus on the setups, not the overall environment. In the right environment, 3 out of 4 stocks will work, and in a bad environment, 3 out of 4 stocks will not work. Even the great setups — if you've experienced enough, you can relate to this — even the great setups don't work. The breakouts fail.

If you judge the environment well, then you can really position yourself when the right environment is about to start and go aggressive, and when you know that the environment has been good for a very long time and the upside is now limited — that's when you change your tactic. You go defensive. You take quicker profits. You invest less money, or you completely close the portfolio and just sit out. That's a high-level skill.

Now in this market my approach is changing. I don't want to sit on my trades for very long. If I'm trading with, let's say, a deeper moving average, now I will trail and protect my profits with a shorter moving average, because I don't care how the individual setup looks. Don't get tempted by how the individual setup is looking. You need to have a broader approach, a broader view, and decide your stance. No matter how tempting the individual setup is, if the weather is changing, I don't care about the single setup. I will change my approach. I will trail with the shorter moving average. You can take bigger profits. For example, earlier you were only booking 25% of the position, and now you can book maybe 75%. That's how you protect your gains.

When things actually change, then you completely go to cash — or the majority of the portfolio sits in cash — and you wait for the right environment. You can see that this time we continue. ## Summary

This is the next level of analysis: judge the right environment. Know how early or how late we are in the trend, and then decide your approach for the coming days. Give importance to the environment over single setups. I'm not saying that in this period there will be no stock that breaks out and explodes — that's not what I'm saying. But if you want odds in your favor, if you want three stocks to work out of four, then this is the kind of market you want to be in early, and this is the kind of market where you do not want to be in aggressive mode once it has run far.
