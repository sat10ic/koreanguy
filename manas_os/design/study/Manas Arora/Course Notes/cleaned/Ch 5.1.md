## Continuation Setups

Continuation is something like this: the stock is going up, takes a pause, goes up again, takes a pause, goes up again. This is a continuation of the trend. The stock broke out, took a pause, continued, paused again, continued, paused again, continued. If you buy during the pause, that's your continuation trade.

### Trade 1 — Parag Milk

Let's start with the continuation. This is my trade, Parag Milk. I bought on June 16th at 110, and this is how it looked when I bought it. You can see 110 on June 16th.

I will explain my setup here. There is an uptrend already. The stock goes up a lot — in this case around 60% in 30 days. Now pay attention to the magnitude and the time it took. In 30 trading days it went up 60%. This number — the magnitude and the duration — is important. You will learn in coming slides why the duration is also very important. It's not just the percentage; it's the duration, how many days the stock has gone up to produce that percentage.

This stock is up 60% in 30 days, then goes down some — profit booking — does not really go down a lot, bounces off around the 20-day moving average, and then after it comes back up it goes really tight.

The next thing is to look at this sideways period of consolidation. I call it **consolidation quality**. In this period, do you really see any red day with a purple dot? No, because there are none. That means there is no big selling happening. Only the purple dots are on the green days, which means: when it goes up, it goes up on heavy volume, but when it comes down, it does not come down on big volume. That's exactly what we want — a stock which has a huge force of buyers and less selling pressure. This is why if the stock breaks out, the likelihood of it continuing the trend is very high.

I've seen a lot of people make the mistake of just focusing on the tightness of the bar. The tightness of the bar is not responsible for the kind of breakout you will get — it is just an entry point. The kind of breakout, the quality of the breakout, the magnitude of breakout, is determined by the force in the trend. This stock is up 58% in the last three months from the lowest point, so there is a strong buying force. This is the reason why, if it breaks out, there is a strong likelihood that you will get a good move, because a lot of pressure is there. Don't make the mistake of only focusing on the tightness and ignoring the consolidation quality, ignoring the buying force. Stronger the force, stronger the result. But there is one catch — which you will learn too: too much force is also not good. I will share those slides showing you what a bad setup looks like.

Anyway, when I saw this and I was tracking it every day, the next morning I selected this in my watchlist and I took the trade the next day. This is how it has been working so far: the stock is up around 28–29% in only nine trading days.

**Continuation five-star conditions — summary:**
1. There has to be a good trend in a reasonable amount of time. In this case, it is up 63% in 31 trading days.
2. It goes down, comes back up, and right around the rising 20 DMA it settles down and gives a very tight day.
3. That is the day I selected in my watchlist for the next day. Next morning I took a position — we'll learn the execution technique later.
4. My main point: do not get lost in the tight days. The whole setup is not just about tight days. It is about there being a good trend and the consolidation quality being very clean.
5. There are no big red days, no purple dots on red days, and this stock — which has a strong buying force of 58% in three months — has gone really tight. That is where you strike, and this one resulted in very good profit.

## Reversal Setups

Never buy a falling knife — you'll cut your hands. This strategy is frowned upon because it's completely against what we've learned in books, that you should never buy a falling stock. I have been doing this for years, and once you are in the club, ask the members: the majority of them are mainly focusing on reversals because that gives you an early entry. The trade is not crowded. I'm going to show you my real trades of how I selected and how I made good money out of reversals.

A reversal is basically this: first you need to understand the main key ingredients. I'm looking for stocks which are in some sort of an uptrend, because — like I said — the kind of move you get after you buy depends on what the prior trend is and how strong it is.

## Key Takeaways: Reversal vs. Continuation

The key takeaways for the reversal setup share common ground with continuation:

**Common to both:**
- You need a strong prior trend — for reversals this means a prior uptrend before the current pulldown.
- Consolidation quality: the fall should be on low volume, no big red days with a purple dot.

**Different for reversal:**
- You need to look for multiple red days together, or a majority of red days out of the last five or six days — for example, five days in a row, or four out of five, or five out of six. When you have so many red days in a cluster, that's your signal to watch for the U-turn.

These are the two main categories into which I have classified my setups.
