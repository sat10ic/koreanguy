## Important source facts preserved

- Edge is not simply a fancy indicator, exceptional chart reading, or the ability to spot a setup.
- Buying tight stocks above a rising **20-DMA** is a strategy component, not the entire edge.
- If the technical setup alone were the edge, most people using 10-DMA, 20-DMA or 50-DMA systems would make money.
- The real edge is the trader's behaviour with outcomes.
- Finding stocks above the 20-DMA can be learned fairly quickly; Manas says one or two months can make a trader decent at the technical side.
- What matters is what the trader does between two great trades: not giving back gains, not losing confidence, not overtrading, not second-guessing.
- Waiting for the right market scenario is edge.
- In poor conditions, the choice is to play small, risk less, or wait.
- In good conditions, the edge includes riding and adding rather than booking too quickly.
- Newcomers tend to book a **5%** gain out of fear; experienced traders may add when conditions and behaviour are right.
- Manas's maturity comes from studying his own trading journal and knowing when he makes easy money.
- In poor environments, he cuts size; in the right environment, he pushes the envelope.
- RK Forge / Ramkrishna Forgings? example preserved: chart of the week did nothing; even a great chart does not guarantee outcome; he used a stop and did not go heavy because environment was not right.
- Mistake sheet process preserved: review yourself daily/weekly, give **+1** or **-1**, record FOMO, chasing, poor entries and poor exits.
- If the mistake sheet does not improve after time, the issue may be the trader, not the market.
- One big mistake can wipe out an otherwise green year; example: green for **11 months**, oversized risk in December, ending the year red.
- Execution is part of edge: taking risk, accepting the loss possibility, entering, riding, staying, and selling neither too early nor too late.
- Example preserved of a person who found great setups for months but never traded them because he feared losing money.
- Three-month test preserved: after three months, the mistake sheet should show far fewer repeated mistakes.

## Book-build caution

This chapter must not become a generic "psychology matters" sermon.

The book version needs to preserve Manas's specific definition of edge:

1. technical setup is necessary but insufficient;
2. waiting for favourable market conditions is edge;
3. cutting risk in poor conditions is edge;
4. riding/adding in good conditions is edge;
5. journaling your own data creates the maturity to know the difference;
6. execution under fear is edge.
