## Stopping at 5–7 Names Intentionally

Now this is how you continue through the list. Let me tell you one thing: in great markets, every day you'll find 20–30 names just in the continuation category, and we haven't even included reversals. If I start with reversals that list will go up even further. In strong markets we are flooded with so many options that we get confused. So I consciously stop when I get some 5–7 names. I consciously stop there and don't look further, because I don't want to get confused. If there are so many stocks moving and breaking out, I don't want a lot of options. I want few options, good options. My job is to make money out of them. If I'm not making money on a consistent basis, that means there's something wrong with my understanding or my stock selection. Having too many options is not good in this business.

So let's say we're done with the list. These are the 11 names in green that I added.

## Cross-Checking on the Weekly Chart

Now I'll tell you about the few names I selected but said I'd explain later why they're not good. If you remember the layout chapter, I have the four-screen layout. I see no red flags on the weekly — the stock is sideways for the last 4 weeks, forming a mini base, not far from the average. This is why I always look at both frames. If the weekly is in disagreement and says the stock is not ready yet, no matter how tempting it looks on the daily, I will avoid it. If you really want to catch a big move, you always have to buy when the stock is forming a base, which is what it is doing now. Once the stock has already done a big run, it is unlikely that it will immediately do the same kind of move again — which is what happened: the stock didn't do anything for the last 4–5 weeks.
