Hi guys, welcome to the last chapter: **What is my daily routine and my final advice to you?**

Why is routine so important?

After interacting with so many successful traders, the one common thing I see is that all of them have a routine. The routine can vary from person to person, but they all have one.

Routine helps because it develops discipline. It develops a sense of responsibility. You take this whole business seriously.

You do not take it casually — one day randomly sitting and finding stocks, then tomorrow placing bids, then suddenly becoming casual and not tracking your trades, not placing your bids regularly, not entering trades in the log sheet, not studying what is working and what is not working, and not finding out what you need to change.

With that casual approach, you are not going to see results.

Just like any other skill, you need a clear routine.

Like bodybuilding: you need a clear routine. You need to lift heavy and have a good diet. If you want to gain weight, you need to be in a surplus. If you want to lose weight, you need a routine.

Trading is the same.

## My evening routine

Every day after the market is over, I scan in the evening.

Then I go through my focus list of around **150 names** and pick around **5–7 names** from it. Then I deliberately and consciously stop there.

I do not find 20 or 30 names every day. On average, it is seven names. It can go to ten or five also, but that is where I draw the line.

I do not go overboard because my idea is not to clutter myself with too many options.

When the market is on and prices are changing every second, the brain freezes and you face issues taking decisions. It is better to have limited options than too many.

This has worked for me.

If you are not able to make money from those 5–7 names, there is something wrong with your selection or your skill level. You need to fix that. Giving yourself too many names will not fix the problem.

That is how I find my names in the evening.

## My morning execution

The next morning, I see whether there is a strong start.

I either place the high bid above the high of the previous day [⚠ raw: “high bit”; likely “buy bid” / “bid above high”] or just buy at market after seeing price action for two or three minutes.

If it is a Busted trade, I place my alerts and then place my bids accordingly.

That is about it.

After that, I intentionally stay away from the live action because there is nothing I can do. I cannot move prices up.

Many of you can relate to this: watching only creates problems. When the price drops very fast, it distracts you from following the plan. You take decisions you are not supposed to take. You stop following the plan.

Imagine if you just woke up and looked at the closing price. Would your life not be much easier than watching the live action?

Yes, in some trades you may catch a top. The stock drops and you feel good. But in the long run, from my experience — and you can ask other successful investors and traders — watching the price every minute, every second, or every few hours has not helped.

It may help in a few trades, but in other trades it will take away the profit you could have made because you sold early.

That is why I stay away from market hours as much as I can. I just keep my alerts on.

Do not put too many alerts either. If you put unnecessary alerts, they bring you back to the screen, which goes against the principle of staying away from the market.

## My weekend routine

On weekends, I go through the strongest groups that are performing in the market.

I check:

- how the groups have changed;
- which group is outperforming;
- which group has stopped outperforming.

I update my focus list with that, because that is the main list. That is the main scan I give more importance to than some new single name in a weak or least-performing group.

If my core focus list, my strong-group list, is updated on the weekend, that list is enough for the entire week to find names.

On weekends, I also study what worked for me and what did not work for me.

I update my stop loss on a daily basis. If my stock is flying higher, my trailing stop goes up, and I move my stops accordingly.

Every day I know my **max pain**.

What is max pain?

Max pain is how much your account will lose if everything goes wrong tomorrow. If every stop gets hit, how much will you lose? That is your open risk. That is your max pain.

You need to know this number.

Once you know this number, trading becomes easier. If tomorrow there is a COVID crash, you know the maximum you can lose. Once you have that knowledge, you will be at peace. When you are at peace, you take better decisions.

That is my daily and weekly routine.

## Protect your mental capital

One line is very important.

On every bad note — if you take a trade and it does not work, or if you interfere with your process and later regret that the stock went higher and now you are not in the trade — no matter what happens, you have to keep feeding positivity to yourself.

As I have said before, this is a lonely business.

No one is really coming for you. You are trading all alone. You need to control your head, protect your mental capital, protect your feelings, and not stay negative.

Every trader goes through a bad phase. Nobody escapes it. Even the biggest investors you study have had drawdowns.

In a drawdown, in a bad phase, you cannot forget the good phase. You cannot forget the skill you have.

Keep feeding positivity to yourself.

If you do negative talk, you bring yourself closer to disaster. You need to prep yourself up. You need to protect yourself.

Again, this is not a teamwork game. It is a solo game.

I consciously practise these things. I stay away from market negativity. I do not read market news much.

All I need is charts, the right time, and my skills.

We have studied the foundations. No matter what happens outside, markets will continue to operate. Today they are down, but there will be a point when markets are down a lot and then they have no choice but to take a U-turn and go up again.

All I have to do is wait for that. Stay positive in the bad phase. My time will come and I will make my money.

That is how I have made money.

Many people try to fit their life into a system. For example, you are a busy person and you try to do intraday trades. It will never work because you are busy, and intraday requires the most screen time.

The ideal thing is to find a system that fits your lifestyle and the amount of time you have in the market.

After one or two years of your own data, you will realise what is working for you and what you need to build on.

When I started, I learned a lot from those books. But today, for example, one book may teach trading with an 8% or 10% stop. Today I trade with less than a 2% stop.

This is how you evolve when you get better. Keep cutting down, keep taking what is working for you, study your data, and then you realise: this is my moment, this is where I am comfortable.

## Losses are the cost of doing business

The last book is really important for mindset [⚠ title not present in transcript text]. I recommend reading it every six months.

It helps you embrace losses.

Losses are nothing but a cost. Every business has costs to run that business. In trading, losses are those costs.

Changing the word — replacing “losses” with “cost of doing business” — shifts your mindset. Suddenly, you do not see losses as negative. You accept them and embrace them. These are the costs of doing business, and you cannot escape them.

You just have to keep them minimum and keep them low.

That book really helped me with this. Read it again and again every six months.

## Final advice

In your learning phase, take smaller trades.

Change your objective from making money to surviving.

Keep studying your performance. Review it. Learn to scale in slowly.

Here is a good exercise.

If you are experienced, you may face difficulty growing from one account size to a bigger account. Suppose you were trading with X account, and now you get extra money and trade with a higher account. You may not be able to take trades the same way because the amount has increased, and the amount you are risking in actual rupee terms is larger.

My tip: never focus on the rupee terms. Always look at how much percentage you are making and how much percentage you are losing.

That way, you train yourself to trade at any account size.

Also, never take a huge jump.

Use progressive overload, like in bodybuilding. When you exercise, you start with 10 kg and then add weight. You cannot jump straight to 50 or 60 kg because your muscle is not ready. You will injure yourself.

Similarly, if you are trading with ₹1 lakh and suddenly start trading with ₹20 lakh, you will face issues because you have not trained your muscle — your head, your mind — to handle that kind of volatility on a ₹20 lakh portfolio.

So gradually increase.

If you are trading with ₹1 lakh and you have ₹20 lakh available, go first to ₹5 lakh, then ₹10 lakh, then ₹15 lakh. Get used to the new amount. Review whether your portfolio is acting well or not, and get better at it.

## Final checklist

Have a routine.

Read the books again and again.

Journal your trades.

Remember the phase you are in.

In the learning phase, take smaller trades. Your idea is to build confidence. Your idea is to survive and master execution, master riding, master trade management, master your head — which is the trickiest part — and master discipline.

Discipline is why everyone is not making money here. If you do not have discipline, you may make money on one trade, but you will give it back on the next trade.
