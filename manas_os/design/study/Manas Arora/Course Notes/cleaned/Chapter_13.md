You must have heard a lot about this already. If you have not, let me tell you exactly what market breadth is.

Market breadth tells you the health of the market: how many stocks are going up and how many are going down.

If more stocks are going up consistently, that is a very healthy market. If more stocks are going down consistently, that tells you the market is in a bad state. A lot of stocks are going down. The majority is going down.

So how does this really help? Is this tool really helpful? How do I use it?

This is the sheet that I maintain. Again, there is no one fixed way to maintain a market breadth sheet. The idea is to find the health of the market.

## The columns in my breadth sheet

I have these columns:

- Number of stocks up more than **4.5%**
- Number of stocks down more than **4.5%**
- Number of stocks up **20% in 5 days**
- Number of stocks above the **20-day moving average** in the entire market
- Number of stocks below the **20-day moving average**
- Number of stocks above the **50-day moving average**
- Number of stocks below the **50-day moving average**

Why 4.5%?

Because some stocks are circuited at 4.98% or 4.97%. To include those stocks, I have set it to 4.5%. There is no other reason.

So this tells me:

- how many are up more than 4.5%;
- how many are down more than 4.5%;
- how many are up 20% in five days;
- how many are above or below the 20-day moving average;
- how many are above or below the 50-day moving average.

Again, there is no one way to know market health. Some people use the 200-day moving average instead of the 50. Some use the 100. Some even use the 10. Every number has its own importance.

I have been managing with this.

Some people also use net new 52-week highs and new 52-week lows: how many stocks are making a new 52-week high versus how many are making a new 52-week low. That is also a very common way to know the health of the market.

If many names are making new 52-week highs, obviously it tells you the market is in good momentum, and vice versa.

## Do not use it for every daily decision

How do I use this? Is it helpful day to day?

The answer is: if you track this sheet every day and make your trading decisions based on this sheet every single day, you will not really be able to trade.

Every other day you will find some warning in some column. It will tell you the market is overheated, and then you will step back and not trade.

The area where it is really helpful is when the markets are at extremes.

You have to spend time on the sheet and find out what the extreme areas are.

## The extreme numbers

You can make this dashboard on your own. You can run screeners for how many stocks are up 5% or down 5%. You can create this in TradingView, and I think Chartink also has a similar market breadth dashboard for free [⚠ raw transcript says “trading also” / “charting also”; likely TradingView / Chartink, confirm if needed].

In my universe, there are only about **1,500–1,600 stocks** that meet my criteria:

- price above ₹30;
- liquid enough to trade.

Out of those 1,500–1,600 names, if already **1,400 stocks** are above the 20-day moving average, that is pretty much everything. The entire crowd is on one side.

If you believe that the large money is usually on the other side, then this matters. You anticipate large money when the entire market is at one extreme.

This year, the lowest number we hit was **298**.

Back in May, June, July 2022, we were at only **50 stocks** above the 20-day moving average.

That is the extreme.

If you want to make a large amount of money, if you want to catch the biggest trend, you need to be on the side where there is no crowd.

If a lot of stocks are refusing to go down, the breadth is also low, the index is refusing to go down, and your trades start working, that is where you can enjoy the move from **49** all the way to **1,400**.

That is where you make easy money.

Not when you are already at 1,465, 1,400, or 1,500 names. At that point, upside is obviously limited.

## What to do when breadth is in the middle

How does this sheet help?

Not when you are already at 800 or 1,100.

If you are somewhere in the middle, do not bother looking at the sheet too much. This sheet is helpful only when you need to decide your approach for the next few weeks.

If you are near 1,500, I become very careful. I am not aggressively trading my trades [⚠ raw phrase: “I’m aggressively trading my trades”; context suggests he means he is *not* aggressive / he becomes defensive at extreme breadth].

If the stocks are super-extended, I am not trailing with the 20-day moving average anymore.

You also need to combine this number with the chart of the benchmark you are following. In my case, that is the mid-cap / small-cap index.

Today we have already hit around **1,400** twice. From May onwards, and now in June, we have hit 1,400 twice in this entire period. Though the market has been advancing, you need to be the smarter person and come out before the market takes over.

That is why I started coming out of a lot of trades in this phase, and I avoided a big hit on my portfolio on those two days.

I am not saying we cannot go really high from here. If you want to build the belief, go back in time and see what happened.

## April 2022 example

The highest number you can see here is **1,500**, and this is April 2022.

If you go back to April 2022, this date is **5 April**. On 5 April, we were already up so much, but then we collapsed.

Again, I am not saying we are going to collapse the same way now. But what I can confidently say is that, without the market taking a pause, I do not see much upside from here in the current scenario.

When you are already at extremes, my approach changes from aggressive to defensive. I will give it a few days to a few weeks for markets to either drop or cool off.

In this phase, a lot of good setups will also crack.

Some people say, “This is a five-star setup. It looks really great. I am tempted to buy it.”

But when the weather changes, even the best ones will crack. They may not crack a lot, but they will not really go. The odds are not in your favour.

Do not hold me down saying, “This stock blasted even though the market was going down.” Yes, that can happen. There can always be something that goes up.

But I am talking about odds. You need to position yourself when things are aligning, when all the stars are aligning with you.

This is another period where we hit around **1,400**. This is January 2022.

Look at what happened. We collapsed. We ran up, hit the extreme number, and it coincided with the market making a double top. Then we fell.

Now let us read the lowest number.

We hit a low number of **90**.

Around **1,200 stocks** were down.

Whenever we see **600–700 names down in one day**, that is also an extreme number.

Historically, this was one of the lowest numbers.

If you see **24 February 2022**, that was the date. Then we started going higher. The next day was a super bounce. After **<u>200 stocks down</u>**[⚠ likely “1,200 stocks down” from the previous sentence, but raw transcript says 200], **740** were up.

The market made a double bottom again. This was like a Busted trade. It refused to go down there.

In this phase, a lot of names exploded. Go and study this period and see how many small-cap and mid-cap stocks exploded.

The mid-cap index was up **17%**. If the index is up 17%, you can imagine what individual stocks did. Stocks usually move way more than the index.

In this period, a lot of stocks must have gone up **20–30%**.

Even in bad market phases, you get rallies where you can grow your account really fast.

That is how I read the market.

## The 20% in five days warning

Sometimes the warning will come from another column.

For example, **127 stocks** were up **20% in five days**. That is really overheated.

The transcript says this was around **7/4/2020** [⚠ likely 7 April 2020, confirm date format]. On the index, you can also see that suddenly the index had four green days in a row. The angle was completely different — almost straight up.

That is overheated.

I had never seen 127 stocks up 20% in five days. Everything was going too much, too fast.

Sometimes the warning will be in this column. Sometimes the warning will be in another column. Most of the time, the most reliable warning is the number of stocks above the 20-day moving average.

But again, not on a daily basis.

I am stressing this: do not use this on a daily basis. Use it only when we have hit the extreme.

Then combine it with index analysis:

- What have the indexes done?
- Are they making a double top?
- Are we hitting resistance in the market?
- Is the market three bases or four bases old?

Then you can change your approach.

## How to change approach at extremes

Do not close all your trades right away.

But you can change your approach.

If you have a very good profitable trade open, instead of trailing with the 20-day moving average, you can move to maybe the 5-day or 10-day moving average. I have already explained how to do that.

If you have a full position open, you can take out **70–80%** profit from it and then trail the remaining **20%** with a smaller moving average.

The third approach is to completely exit, but I would not recommend that immediately, because markets can stay extended. The market can stay at 1,400 for a very long time.

Markets make bottoms very fast. The **90/97** type readings will not usually stay for long [⚠ raw transcript says “the 97 will not really stay for long”; earlier low was 90].

Historically, you can verify that markets have stayed in the **1,300–1,500** breadth zone for days [⚠ raw transcript says “13 to 1500 trades”; cleaned as 1,300–1,500 breadth zone].

So just because you see 1,300 hitting on day one does not mean you should close your portfolio and assume you will not see your stocks breaking out.

Markets can stay there. You can see 1,400 stayed for many days, then again came to 1,400. From 1,400 we took a dip, some minor correction, and then hit 1,400 again.

This tendency is very normal.

If you hit that number for the first time, do not panic. Do not close everything. Just change your approach from fifth gear to third gear.

Then, if a lot of positions are getting closed, come down to first gear.

If the remaining stocks also start closing, if trailing stops are getting hit, then come down to neutral gear.

Then wait for breadth to cool off.

Once breadth has cooled off, ignore it. Do not keep watching the breadth sheet again and again. Focus on what the indices are doing, what the sectors are doing, and find stocks with relative strength [⚠ transcript appears to drop a clause at the end after “relative”].
