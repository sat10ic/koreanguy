Hi guys, welcome to the next important chapter: **how to spot and stay ahead with outperforming sectors**.

How do you find out which sector is doing better than the other sector? Is it really helpful to stay with the strong sector, or is it just a myth?

This is a tweet from 2019 where I said that three stocks — Dixon, **Hyreg**[⚠ likely Havells? / HIRECT? ticker unclear], and Orient Electric — from consumer electronics had broken out of their long-term bases around the same time.

The key is **around the same time**.

You get large moves when the entire sector moves together, as chemicals did in 2016.

This is where your money should go.

When one sector has six, seven, or ten names, and the majority — 60–70% — were down and then took a U-turn and started moving together, that is where you should focus your money.

Staying in a sector where only one name is going up is different from staying in a sector where a large number of names are going up together. When many names move together, you get bigger moves and smoother moves. Even if the market stops going up, that group can fight the downtrend pretty well because there is relative strength in the sector.

That helps you find good trades from that sector.

## Why sector strength matters

I created this video sometime in February 2023. In that video, I showed how I studied the strongest group and positioned myself.

Today we are in June 2023. Many names discussed in that February video have gone up **50–100%**.

That is real evidence that staying with the strongest sector, the strongest group, paid off.

If the entire group is performing, you get bigger moves. When many stocks from one group are moving, you have better odds of making money versus entering a group where only one stock is performing.

With that idea, every weekend I find which groups are outperforming. Based on that, I select all the stocks from that group and put them in my watchlist.

That watchlist is over and above the other watchlists and scans you can follow, like the one-month-high scan or the 52-week-high scan.

If you get enough names from the group list itself, there is no need to go deeper and find stocks from other scans, because the best quality is already available. It is better to have limited names in your watchlist than too many options, which will distract you and may leave you making no money at all.

## Start with MarketSmith groups

The first thing I do is go to MarketSmith.

I have a subscription. It is not very expensive. They usually run some offer every other day. Right now I think it is available for around ₹2,000 or ₹3,000 per year.

When you log in to MarketSmith:

1. Click **Resource Tools**.
2. Go to **Idea List**.
3. On the left side, go to the **Markets** tab.
4. Under Markets, click **197 Industry Groups**.

These are 197 groups created by MarketSmith.

Some groups are absolutely very small. For example, one group may have a market cap of only ₹160 crore for the entire group and only three stocks. There is no point tracking such groups because they are illiquid. You will not really get the right position size even if you spot a very good chart. So there is no point wasting time there.

What I do is sort the groups in descending order by market cap — highest market cap first.

After sorting this way, I look at groups with enough stocks. For example, if there are 34 stocks in a group, I check what has happened in the last three months.

Suppose three months ago the group was ranked 37, then dropped to 47 — that is not good. But if from last week to this week there is a good jump in rank, the group has improved.

Anything around rank 40 or under 40, I like to track. I check the names in that group and see if anything interesting is happening.

## Compare each stock with the market

On the top chart, I have the mid-cap/small-cap index, which has around **400 stocks**. On the lower chart, I open Bank of Baroda.

Now give it not more than five seconds and ask: do you see relative strength?

When I say relative strength, I mean: is the stock doing better than the market?

You can clearly see that Bank of Baroda is making higher lows while the index was recently making lower lows. The stock price is above the 20-DMA, while the index is still below the 20-DMA. So it is doing slightly better than the market.

This is relative strength.

Relative strength can be tricky at first. But with practice, if you give it two or three seconds, you will be able to guess whether it is doing better than the market or not.

Since Bank of Baroda is doing better, I add it to my watchlist and move on.

## Which groups to open

One more thing: in every group, you only open where you see a strong jump in rank.

For example:

- Oil & Gas was ranked **103** three months ago. Now it is **23**. That is a strong jump, and I want to study it.
- Finance Consumer Loans was ranked **90** three months ago. Now it is **68**. That is also a good jump.
- Cosmetics was **112**, now **82**. Good jump.
- Auto was **130**, now **80**. I want to study it.

What I do not want to study is something like Insurance: it was **125**, then **111**, now **115**. It is better than three months ago, but it has only two stocks. I do not want to study this because only one stock may be doing well and improving the rank.

Another example: three months ago a group was ranked **9**, which was very high. Now it is **156**. I do not want to study this group. That is not growth; that is deterioration. Stay away from studying this group.

Only open groups where you see improving rank — either versus three months ago or versus last week. If there is a reasonable jump, study it.

Then open the group, sort by Buyer Demand or by market cap, and select the best names.

## The final strong-group list

After going through all the groups that improved in rank based on last week’s performance or the three-month performance, I create the final list.

Do not get lost in tiny number differences. If a group was 43 last week and now 44, that is still all right. It is still around the top 40. You need reasonable acceptance.

Go through those groups and see which names have outperformed.

Finally, I have a list that I feel has good bias against the entire market [⚠ raw phrase: “good bias against the entire market”; likely means good relative strength versus the market].

Then I go one by one.

One name was in a strong trend, dropped, and is trying to go up again. Not very strong, but all right.

Another is a no-brainer — absolutely easy to guess. I have a position from that day and have been holding for many days. Now it is setting up another entry. This also has strong RS.

One name is not strong anymore, so I delete it.

Another was in a downtrend and is now in an uptrend.

This way, I select names. All these names, after going through the entire group exercise, are names that have shown outperformance against the market.

Every weekend I make this list. For the rest of the week, every evening, I find trade opportunities from this list.

If I have five to seven names that are ready for tomorrow, I do not go beyond this list. That is enough. I need five to seven names, and I need one or two trades from that list.

If I fill my list with 15–20 names, the next morning I will be puzzled and confused about what to go after.

So do not clutter yourself with too many options.
