## Case pattern: tight day before the move

In the first examples, Manas repeatedly points to the same structure:

- the previous day’s candle is very small;
- the stock is tight;
- the next day gives a Strong Start;
- the resulting move is good.

He counts the cases as he goes.

In the first four examples, he sees the same thing: a small previous-day range before the successful Strong Start.

Then five out of five show the same idea.

Then six out of six.

In another example, the previous day is not as small, so he treats it more carefully. At that point he says it may be **six out of seven**.

The repeated point is that before getting a good **10-20%** move, the day before the Strong Start was often very tight.

## The exception: down five days is a different setup

One example is not tight in the same way.

Manas still selected it for discussion because the stock had been down for around **five days**. In that case, the Strong Start was working from a different structure. It was not the same “extreme tight previous day” setup.

So he does not count it in the same tightness sample.

This matters because he is not trying to force every chart into one rule. A stock coming after five down days belongs to a different category from a stock resting tightly for one day and then expanding.

## Seven out of eight, then nine out of ten

## The invalid Strong Start: buying on the fourth green day

One submitted example is technically a Strong Start and it worked, but Manas calls it invalid for his process.

The reason: the person bought on the **fourth green day in a row**.

The argument from the trader was that this stock, likely DLF based on the raw transcript, usually moves for **six-seven days**. Manas rejects that reasoning.

His point is:

Why do you want to pocket only the remaining two days and enter in the middle of the move?

Even if that trade works, it develops the wrong habit. It trains the trader to ignore the previous three or four green days and still buy. One day, that habit will take away the gains from several lucky trades.

So he does not count this trade in the sample. It may have been a Strong Start, and it may have worked, but he does not want to make money the wrong way.

The process matters because habits compound too. Sadly, so do stupid habits. The market is very democratic that way.

## The conclusion: more than 80% support the point

Manas closes the study by saying these were members’ wins, so there was no cheating in the sample.

Before a good Strong Start result, the previous day was usually extremely tight.

When there is extreme tightness, and the next day gives a Strong Start with great morning volume, the odds of getting a strong move improve.

This does not guarantee anything. It improves the odds.

## How to use the observation

If a stock does not have a great tight day before the entry, maybe it should not be included in the next day’s watchlist.

There is no fixed number for “how tight.” Manas does not give a percentage threshold.

Instead, he says to memorize the pictures.

Compare the size of the current day with the previous days of the same stock. If the current day is the tightest action in many days, that is meaningful.

The tighter the stock is, the more it can improve the odds of a good Strong Start.

The reason is volatility behaviour.

A stock goes through:

- volatility contraction;
- volatility expansion.

When activity dries to a very low level, there is less room for further dryness. The next likely phase is expansion.

If the earlier momentum was upward, and the stock contracts tightly, then expansion in the direction of the prior upward momentum becomes the opportunity.

This information helps eliminate weak names from the watchlist and improves selection quality.

The practical rule is:

Do not just ask, “Is this a Strong Start candidate?”

Ask:

1. Was the previous day extremely tight relative to this stock’s own recent bars?
2. Is the next morning showing Strong Start behaviour with volume?
3. Am I buying early, or am I buying the fourth green day and calling it skill?

That third question is where many accounts quietly go to receive spiritual education.
