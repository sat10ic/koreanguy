## Entry Category 1: Strong Start

I have classified my entries into two broad categories. The first is called **Strong Start** (SS). As the name suggests: I buy something if I have five to seven names in my list and tomorrow I see a strong start in them. I will enter and will not wait for the highest breakout point in the range.

### Example 1: Parag Milk (16th June)

I bought Parag Milk on 16th June and I bought it here. Let's study this. On 15th June in the evening, when I was scanning for good setups, I found this stock. If you've carefully watched the setup in the watchlist video, this is the great setup I covered — a clear trend that goes down, goes up, and goes really tight. The quality of consolidation is really clean and the buying force is 58%. It is 58% up from the lowest point in three months.

The next morning I had selected this in my watchlist. I'm not buying it the first thing in the morning. I need to see my entry method, which is Strong Start.

**What is Strong Start?** The stock closed at 107.80, which is where I've drawn a line. The highest point of that day is 109.85, where I drew another line. Tomorrow, if I see Parag Milk opening anywhere between this line or even above the top line, that's the Strong Start. I need:
- A gap-up opening.
- The low of the next day should not reach the close of the previous day.

When I say "should not," there can be reasonable breaches. If it breaches by some ticks — 20-30 — that's all right. If the low is 107.8 and it goes to 106, that is not a reasonable breach; that is a clear breach. Some reasonable breach is acceptable, but if you get a gap-up and absolutely no breach, that's the best strong start you can expect.

After gapping up and not reaching the close, the price goes higher than the previous day's high (the upper red line). In layman's terms: the next day, buyers gapped up the price and were so strong in numbers that sellers failed to drop the price back even to the previous close. Closing price is the agreement price — where buyers and sellers agreed to close that day.

The next day, the fight between buyers and sellers starts again, but sellers today fail to even break the previous close. Buyers are so strong they take the price higher and above the previous day's high. That is the Strong Start.

The second ingredient — not mandatory, but it makes the choice easy: **relative volume (RVOL)**. If the stock also shows some good volume in the first two to three minutes, that's a bonus. If you have four names for tomorrow and two are starting strong but only one has volume backing, the choice is clear. If in the first two minutes you've already seen 8-10% of average daily volume come in, you know by end of day the volume will be really high.

**How to execute Strong Start:**
- Wait for two to three minutes after the open (9:15 → 9:17 or 9:18).
- By 9:18 you can already see whether the previous close is holding.
- If price is holding above the previous close, place a bid above the previous day's high.

**What happened the next day:** We had a gap-up opening. Clearly, the price opened right above the previous day's high. The low of the day never came remotely close to the previous close. At 9:17, the price was 111. The previous close was not being broken and the price was already above the previous day's high. I bought at market.

**Stop placement:**
- For beginners: use the previous day's close, or a max of 4%.
- Eventually, after some 20-30 trades, reduce the stop to the day's low (which in this case was less than 2%).
- If the day's low is more than 2.5%, default to a 2.5% stop.

I recommend starting with the wider stop because you are in a learning phase — not an earning phase. Frequent losses with tight stops will erode confidence and could make you quit.

I bought Parag Milk at almost 111. The stock is up around 33% in only 10 trading days. Even with a 4% stop, that's almost 1:8 risk-reward. If you bring the stop to 2%, you're looking at 1:15 trades. I've even had 1:50 and 1:200 — like the BSC [⚠ unverified ticker] trade in 2021, where I bought on 15th November with a half-percent stop and the stock went up 65%. More than 100 times the risk.

## Summary: Strong Start Checklist

- There has to be a gap at the opening the next day. **Avoid** if the gap is already 5-6% — it will disturb your risk-reward. In that case, pass on the trade.
- At the low of the next day (the SSA [⚠ ASR garble: raw was "SSA" — likely "the low"] should not breach the previous day's close. Ideally open = low. Minor breaches (touch and go) are allowed, but not a convincing break.
- Wait for two to three minutes. If you have these two conditions, buy above the previous day's high. If you can't be at the screen at open, place a bid and move away.
- Relative volume in the first two minutes is a bonus, not a requirement.

Note: Strong Start may not suit someone who is really busy around opening hours. The other methods we'll cover work for people struggling to combine work and trading.

## Entry Category 2: Pullback, Reversal, and Busted

I've classified my second broad entry method into:

1. **Pullback entry:** The stock is already in an uptrend, comes down a bit, and you enter in that dip.
2. **Reversal:** Like Bsoft — the stock comes down and you use Strong Start to enter the reversal.
3. **Busted:** A form of reversal, but played on a 15-minute frame at specific reaction levels.

## Busted Entry Method (Reversal on a 15-Minute Frame)

The most important method for me is **Busted** — technically a reversal, but played differently.

### Busted Example 1: Zen Technologies (multiple positions from Feb)

One trade I started buying in February was Zen Technologies (Corrected; raw: "Zentec") at 215. I created multiple positions — six, maybe eight or nine entries total.

**First entry at 215:** Strong Start method (already explained). But what about the entries after 215?

The stock had come out of a big base. On the weekly it was a very fresh breakout. The stock had previously gone from 97 to 234 in only a few days — I know its nature, it is a beast and moves really fast. Market conditions were also turning favorable (markets had stopped going down). I decided I did not want to close anything and would just look for ways to buy more.

Zen Technologies started going higher. It went to almost 260 but I could not create an entry — I was waiting for a pullback. After two massive red days (down around 11%), I switched to a 15-minute frame.

**How the Busted method works:**

On the 15-minute chart, identify the most important reaction levels (support points) where you want to see how the stock reacts. You are not saying buy blindly at support — you are watching for a strong reaction.

1. **Place an alert** at the important support level.
2. **Once that first alert triggers:** it only means the level was breached. Place a **second alert** above the high of the bar that breached that level.
3. The second alert must trigger within the **next 2-3 bars (30-45 minutes)**, sooner the better. If it does not trigger in that window, remove the trade — it is not a good Busted setup.

**Why urgency matters:** If this level is really important, what are the buyers waiting for? They got the price. Why are they not punching large orders? If the reaction is slow — not coming in the next 2-3 bars — the quality of that reversal goes down sharply. All the reversals I've made money on had this most important ingredient: urgency.

**What happened on 24th February (entry at 237):**

The stock was at 238 and the important level was at 231. I placed an alert at 231. The next day the level was breached; alert triggered. I placed my second alert above the high of that breach bar. In the next bar I got a massive strong bounce — exactly the kind of reaction I'm looking for. Sellers got trapped; buyers came in strong. I bought at 237 on February 24th. My stop was below that range — from 237, that was around 232.5, roughly a 2-2.5% stop.

The stock closed the day at 268. I did not sell anything.

**Entry 3 — 13th March (at 263):**

Stock came down for three days in a row after February 24th. Same logic as Bsoft — reversal opportunity. I was on the 12th of March chart, stock already coming down. I identified the important prior support zone where the stock had bounced previously. I put an alert at 260.75 (the lowest prior point). That alert triggered — but this time, once the level got released, a lot of supply came in and the stock came crashing down. Not a problem. The first alert only means: go place the second alert, not to take action.

I placed my second alert at 262.35 (the high of the breach bar). In the next bar itself there was a strong U-turn. I bought at 263. The third position — still holding all three.

**Entry 4 — 16th March (at 285.50):**

On 15th March I was tracking the stock on the 15-minute chart. There was one important reaction point where the stock had bounced very strongly previously. Alert got active. There was a breach. I placed my next alert above the high of that bar. I waited — first bar, second bar — clearly this level was not getting triggered even after 30 minutes. I waited for one more bar, and that never happened. So no trade at that level.

Then I found another important swing low. Calculated from there. That level triggered. I placed my alert slightly higher — because the two bars were very tiny and I wanted a convincing bounce, not mediocre. The alert triggered in the second bar. That is why I bought more on this day. Fourth entry. That is how I created multiple positions in Zen Technologies — that is what Busted is.

## Summary: Busted Method

Busted will work well for someone who is busy and cannot be at the screen — but you will have to place alerts:

1. Place an alert at the most important reaction level you see on the 15-minute chart.
2. Once the first alert triggers, place a second alert (or a bid) above the high of that breach bar. Do not take action on the first alert alone.
3. If your bid does not execute within 30-45 minutes, cancel it and move on. The quality goes bad after that window.

**Key rules:**
- The daily setup must be great before you even go to the 15-minute frame. Do not buy garbage stocks just because a Busted pattern appeared on a 15-minute chart.
- Once the daily is in sync, use the 15-minute to get in earlier with a tighter stop.
- The most obvious reaction points will stand out. If you have confusion about whether a level is important, it is probably not important.
- The stock must have bounced really well from that point historically.
- Sooner is better — the ideal is when the second alert triggers on the same bar as the breach, or the very next bar.

## Final Summary: My Two Entry Types

1. **Strong Start** — buy at open when the stock gaps up above the previous close and holds.
2. **Pullback / Reversal / Busted** — enter during a controlled dip using 15-minute reaction levels with the alert-based system.
