### Why Magnitude Differs: Market Cap

Why? Because the duration — the first foundation — is the same on every frame, on every instrument, on every market, Indian or American or any third market. But the magnitude, the percentage, is not the same. We can always find a range, but every stock moves very differently.

The reason why we have different ranges is because of size: small caps, mid caps, and large caps.

If the stock is a small cap — which means only a few shares are available in the market, meaning very small supply is available — even a little demand can really explode the stock and it can move really fast. We have seen that, which is why Elecon had a range of 40–50% also. Paytm does not have that range at the lower end, because if you went to market cap, it is going to be definitely more than Elecon.

The point I'm trying to make here is that every stock has a different way of moving. We can judge it by looking at the past: if a stock has done this kind of a move before — if it is up 29% in four days — it tells you the nature of the stock. It can always repeat that in the coming future, which is why you can see 15% in three days. If I know this, from a tiny red day it went up almost 28% in eight days of similar behavior. Looking at the past, we can find out the range for that stock and take an average. We can never know for certain that it will go to 50% in three or four days, but we can get an idea.

## CH 2.3: Market Cycles and Stage Analysis

The next important information that you need to really remember is market cycles and how a stock can go through these four stages. This information can really help you in identifying where we stand in the trend: whether we are really early in the trend, the trend is really topping out, or it's too late already and the upside is limited.

### The Four Stages — Example 1

This is a weekly chart of a very popular stock [⚠ unverified ticker — context says "a very popular stock in London from 2018"; ticker not stated in transcript] from 2018. If you see this, it was absolutely in no trend. If I draw a box here, it was sideways and the moving averages were also not really trending.

All of a sudden — this period is 7 years. This looks like a very small box but this is 7 years — something happened. All of a sudden the stock started trending and the stock just went up from 150 to 5,000 in only — this is Jan 17 and this is August 18 — a period of only 15–16 months. The stock was up some 30 times. It was dead for 7 years and then it went up 30 times in only 15–16 months.

After that it went sideways for a while. Then price collapsed: it broke both moving averages and had a sharp fall — almost from 5,000 back to almost where it started, around 400. This is a complete cycle. This is what I'm talking about: Stage 1, Stage 2, Stage 3, and Stage 4.

- **Stage 1:** No trend for many months or even many years — the basing period.
- **Stage 2:** The price crosses both averages. The shorter moving average is above the longer one, the price is trending up, and you see an explosive rise — the stock usually goes 2–3×, 10–20×, or even 30× as in this case.
- **Stage 3:** The price has already gone up a lot and is no longer progressing much. It becomes really choppy. The largest rise was from Jan 17 to Jan 18; after that from Jan 18 to November 18 the price did not really progress much. It's not really the best time to buy.
- **Stage 4:** The price collapses — down almost 80–90%.

### The Four Stages — Example 2

I can give you one more example. This is another weekly chart. Stage 1 is where the stock had no trend and was just forming a base. The prices were going above and below the averages, and then the range breakout happened.

Stage 2: price started going up rapidly. The averages are trending up and the prices are advancing really fast. The stock goes from 42 to 700 in only two years. In only two years the price went up from 40 to 700.

After that, the price started to top out. Finally the price kept going below the averages convincingly, and then Stage 4 set in — the price collapsed back to where it started, almost 80–90%. Again a complete cycle: base (Stage 1), advance (Stage 2), top-out formation (Stage 3), rapid decline (Stage 4).

We do not have to identify Stage 1, Stage 3, or Stage 4. We are just concerned with Stage 2, because that is where you make money the fastest. You can see the stock went up from 50 to 700. This is exactly where you can grow your account really fast.

How is this information helpful? If you can count, and if the base count is really obvious, and the stock is already three bases old — the upside after that is very limited, and a lot of false breakouts are possible.

This is why the base count is really important. You can find out how early you are in the trend. If you're here — only two bases old — that's just the start of the move.
