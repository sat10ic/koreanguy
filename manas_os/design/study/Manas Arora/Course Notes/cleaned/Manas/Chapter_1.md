## Why this comes first

Hi guys, welcome to the first chapter: **Fear Management and the Road to Successful Investing**.

This is where we begin this entire course, *Fast and Fortunate: How to Grow Your Small Account Fast*. There is a reason I have kept this right at the beginning.

Usually, everything starts with creating a scan and looking for great setups: what is the fancy indicator that can really print money for you, how to buy, where to sell, what the stock should be. The entire talk is around these factors.

Right at the end, there will be talks on mindset and psychology. In my experience, by the time you reach the last few chapters and the last few important aspects, you are so busy and so excited with the technical side that you do not really pay attention to mindset and risk management.

How many times have you noticed that you have done a great amount of analysis, but when the time comes to execute the trade, you are scared? So many things come into your head: the markets are down, I should risk small. Even though your setup is really good, you risk small.

Or you take a normal-sized trade. It goes up, then starts coming down. You are scared to lose what you are making, so you panic and press the sell button.

That fear is there.

Can you really walk on a thin rope if you are scared? If you try walking on a thin rope for the first time, you know you will fall. And you will fall. You need practice. You need to practise on your fear.

Walking on a rope starts with a net below you. If you fall, the net protects you. Slowly and gradually, after a lot of practice, you remove the net, you get better at it, and confidence comes in.

Here, every mistake will cost you money. So the first thing you need to do is control and manage your fear. Otherwise, no scan can really help you. Finding a great trade cannot help you.

You will like this space, but every time the stock comes down, you will not survive this space, and you will never see the stock going up again.

I get this message: “My stock goes up 5%, and it retraces back to 2%. I could not handle the volatility, so I closed the trade. Then I saw my stock flying back up again.”

What you need is not a fancy trading system that tells you where to buy and where to sell. First, you need to solve the problems in your head. You need to build the muscle.

First, do the fear management.

## Learning phase and thriving phase

How do you do that?

First, know that there are two phases in your journey to successful investing:

1. **Learning phase**
2. **Thriving phase**

Once you know what phase you are in, your job becomes slightly easier because you do not keep unrealistic expectations for that phase.

In the learning phase, you cannot have the expectations of the thriving phase. Many people without training are in the learning phase, but behave as if they are in the thriving phase.

For example, just as I was making this video, somebody commented below my tweet: “If one wants to make big, one has to bet a huge amount on a single stock.”

I agree — but do you really know what I am saying?

You first need the skill of handling what is handed over to you when you are betting a huge amount on a single stock. If you do this in your learning phase, you will clear your account. That is a recipe for disaster.

So knowing the phase is really important.

Even if you have previous knowledge, I want you to treat yourself as a new person. In the learning phase, change your goal from making money to surviving.

As long as you survive for the first 30 to 50 trades, even if you are down, say, 5% or 10%, that is all right. That 5–10% is the fee you are paying to learn the market and build confidence that what you are learning actually works.

Once you get that confidence, once you get the strong belief that no crash can wipe you out, that is when you move from the learning phase to the thriving phase.

You cannot start by taking big trades. You cannot start by going all-in on one stock. For the first 30 to 50 trades, remember that you know nothing. You are going to make your own mistakes. Every mistake will cost you.

If you lose all your chips, how will you stay to make money in the thriving phase?

So in the learning phase, stay small. Take smaller risks. It is all right; you will make mistakes. Your goal is to reduce those mistakes after every few trades. Review your trades and you will get better.

Once you develop the strong belief that yes, you can find good trades — and not only find great trades, but manage them and ride them well — then you know you have strong risk management in place.

You are not scared of cutting losses at your stop level. You are not scared of seeing profits evaporate. You are not interfering with the process. You are only taking money either when you are getting stopped out, or when the stocks get super extended — which we will learn in the how-to-sell, how-to-exit, and ride-management chapters.
