Hi guys, welcome to the new chapter: **Understanding Your Edge**.

Many experienced traders here would have heard this term: your system, or your trading system, needs to have an **edge**.

But what exactly is edge?

Is it relying on some fancy technical indicator? Is it exceptional chart-reading skill?

Every day on Twitter, Manas gets tagged by people who find new names and new setups emerging. He does not blame them. In the beginning, all of us feel that finding a great setup, or having great chart-reading skill, is some great discovery. It feels as if we have cracked the market.

But the reality is far away from that.

This chapter explains what Manas believes his edge is, and what your edge needs to become if you want to make money consistently from stocks.

## Edge is an advantage, but not where most people look

An edge is an advantage. It is a strong point that helps you make money in the market.

But many people mistake edge for something purely on the strategy side.

For example, someone may say:

> “My strategy is that I buy stocks that are tight and above the rising 20-DMA. That is my edge.”

Manas says that is not really the edge.

If buying stocks above the 20-DMA were the only edge, then it would no longer be hidden. A lot of people already know that strong momentum stocks above the 20-DMA can work.

Some traders buy around the 10-DMA for faster momentum. Some buy around the 50-DMA. If the technical side alone were responsible for making money, then the majority of people following any reasonable moving-average system would make money.

But that is not what happens.

So if the setup is not the full reason, what separates people who make money from the majority who do not?

In Manas's opinion, the answer is **you**.

Your edge is how you behave with the outcomes of a stock.

## Finding setups is not the hard part

Finding stocks above the 20-DMA is not hard.

When Manas started the training, he said you can become pretty good on the technical side in one or two months. You do not need a fancy education to learn how to find stocks above the 20-DMA.

Finding good trades, finding stocks above the 20-DMA, and finding stocks breaking out is not the difficult part.

The difficult part is what you do **between** two great trades.

Suppose you find one great trade and make good money.

Then, before the next great trade arrives, you take five or six trades. If you mess up during that period, give back all your gains, lose confidence and start second-guessing, then the next great trade only recovers your old losses.

So finding the first good trade and the sixth good trade is not the whole game.

The game is what you do between them.

Not doing anything stupid is edge.

Not giving back gains unnecessarily is edge.

Not losing confidence during ordinary periods is edge.

Not second-guessing your system at the exact moment it needs patience is edge.

The market does not usually destroy traders with one missing indicator. It destroys them with five ordinary decisions made while they were bored, irritated, overconfident or trying to “just recover yesterday’s loss.” Very professional. Very MBA. Very bankrupt if repeated.

## Waiting for your time is edge

Manas refers to a market-breadth scenario ⚠ where the environment is not the place where he makes easy money.

When you know that the market is not conducive for big moves, the mistakes you make in that phase are often the reason you do not make good money.

If you wait for your days, that is the real edge.

Waiting for your time is entirely in your hands. Nobody is going to come and guide you every day. Trading is a lonely business. Every day, you have to tell yourself what the conditions are and what the current situation is.

When Manas knows that the market is not favourable for big moves, he has a choice:

- play very small;
- avoid risking much;
- lose very little even if trades fail;
- or, if he has enough control, wait for conditions to improve.

Then, when the favourable scenario comes, he knows that bigger moves are more likely.

To say it again: finding great trades is not difficult. What you do between two great trades is the reason you make money or do not make money.

The edge is not simply finding stocks above the 20-DMA.

The edge is waiting for the scenario where that 20-DMA setup works well.

## Riding is also edge

Even when the right market arrives, edge is not only buying at the right place.

The edge is also buying at the right place and then not taking profit too soon.

When conditions are right, you may get **20%**, **30%** or **40%** moves more easily. That is the period you waited for.

But when the time finally comes, if you take profits too quickly, that is not edge.

A newcomer buys a stock, sees it up **5%**, and immediately wants to lock the gain. He is scared of giving back that 5%.

An experienced trader looks at the same situation differently. He has waited for this market. He knows the odds have improved. Instead of rushing to exit, he looks for ways to add more if the trade behaves correctly.

The beginner says, “Profit dikh raha hai, nikaal lo.”

The professional says, “This is the period I was sitting through all that boring nonsense for. Why am I running away now?”

This condition will not last forever. The trader has to compensate for the waiting period.

Buying more can be edge.

Riding the move can be edge.

The edge is not only the technical pattern.

## The edge is maturity

Manas says the difference between him and someone struggling is not that he has discovered some magical technical secret.

The difference is maturity.

He has the maturity to wait for the right days. That maturity came because he studied the right days a lot.

He recorded his trades in his trading journal. Based on that data — his own data, which is the most valuable data for him — he studied when he makes easy money.

After studying that data, he realised:

- this is the right environment where I make easy money;
- when that environment is absent, I do not make easy money;
- when conditions are poor, I must cut down my size;
- when the right time comes, I can push the envelope and take bigger trades;
- instead of booking early, I can find ways to buy more and go all out.

That is the edge.

This is why, when people tag him on Twitter with a chart, he understands their excitement. Newer traders think finding a great setup is the exciting part.

But finding a setup is a very small part of the game.

## The RK Forge example

Manas gives the example of **RK Forge / Ramkrishna Forgings?** ⚠.

He says he called it the chart of the week and shared it in the MiMo / club channel ⚠.

But what happened?

It did not do anything the whole week.

If “chart of the week” meant guaranteed outcome, he would have gone all in without a stop and expected the best result.

But in reality, even a great chart does not guarantee an outcome.

So he was prepared with a stop, and he did not go heavy even though it was the chart of the week.

Why?

Because he knew it was not the right environment.

Those decisions are edge.

He does not blow up when things are not right. He holds on to his chips and waits.

The edge is everywhere because the edge is him.

In your case, the edge is you.

## Review yourself, not only charts

If you want to get better, you have to work on yourself.

You can maintain a mistake sheet.

Every day, traders review charts:

- How was the day?
- How did the stock behave?
- Which stocks moved?

But how many traders review themselves?

Ask:

- Did I do anything stupid today?
- Did I do anything great today?
- Did I chase because of FOMO?
- Did I buy at the right point?
- Did I take profit at the right time?
- Did I take profit too soon?
- Did I take profit too late?

If you did something right, give yourself a **+1**.

If you did something wrong, give yourself a **-1**.

Keep writing down the mistakes you make.

If you are new, the sheet will initially have many mistakes. That is normal. The goal is that, with time, the sheet should become nearly blank.

If it does not become cleaner, then maybe trading is not for you.

Because if you know your mistakes, and two months later you are still committing the same mistakes, then it is not the market.

It is you.

If you are not willing to work on yourself, this game is not for you.

## One mistake can take away the year

Trading is a very hard business.

Why?

Because no matter how good you have been for months, one mistake can take away your gains.

Suppose it is December. For the first **11 months**, you were green.

Then, in December, you go all out and take much bigger risk than you should. You end the year in red.

That will dent your confidence. If you are new, you may even leave trading because the whole year was good and then ended horribly.

Knowing that you can give up your gains in one go is important.

That realisation stops you from acting stupid.

That is edge.

So you need to work on yourself every day. Review your actions every week and check whether you are actually getting better.

## Execution is part of edge

Manas is not denying that the system matters.

Where to buy matters.

Strong Start matters.

The right setup matters.

Execution matters.

But he feels that most people already know what a good setup looks like. That is not the biggest problem anymore.

The question is:

Do you really wait for the right setup?

Have you taken trades when the setup was not right?

If you did not wait, you do not have edge.

Again, it is you.

This game is not only charts. It is not finding a great setup and getting excited over it.

## The setup-finder who never traded

Manas gives another example without naming the person.

This person was very good at finding setups. Among the few people Manas knew, he was genuinely good at finding names, and those names performed well.

For three or four months, the person kept finding and sharing great setups with excitement.

One day, Manas asked him:

> “How much are you up?”

The person said:

> “I have never traded them.”

Manas was shocked.

Why was he finding setups every day if he was not trading them?

The person said he was scared to lose money.

This, Manas says, is the perfect example of no edge.

The person had chart-finding ability but no execution. If you are great at finding setups but your execution is zero, there is no edge.

Execution means:

- taking the risk;
- accepting that you have taken the risk;
- being okay with losing that money on that trade;
- finding the trade;
- taking the trade;
- riding it;
- staying in it;
- not booking too early;
- not booking too late.

Behind every step, it is you.

## The three-month test

If this is a serious business for you, start a mistake sheet.

Every day, review yourself.

Write the mistakes there.

In the next three months, that mistake sheet should have very few mistakes.

If you are still committing the same mistakes after three months, Manas says you should seriously consider whether this business is for you.

Otherwise, you will keep moving in circles and not really make money.

Thank you.
