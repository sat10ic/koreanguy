## The Gap-Down Fear

I get a lot of questions like this — a very common one: "What if you buy a stock and tomorrow there is a gap down, and it gaps down beyond your stop?"

I don't blame you for this question. It is a very legitimate concern. But the reason you're constantly worried about it is that you have no idea about position sizing. In this chapter you will learn that no gap down can ever take you out.

Position sizing is a crucial aspect of trading that involves determining the right size of a trade based on:
- The amount of capital you have
- Your level of risk tolerance
- How the market conditions are

---

## Why Position Sizing Must Change With Market Conditions

If you remember the market environment chapter: if you trade with just one approach throughout the year, there will be months where you make a lot of money and months where you give up all of that money — because you are not adapting to changing conditions.

There are certain environments where some trades are really working. It feels so easy: you take a trade and it goes 20-30% up in no time, and you find so many trades in a row. Then suddenly, things change. The weather changes. There are a lot of false breakouts and the upside is limited.

If you don't change the approach — if you're still risking the same amount, still taking the same size — then obviously in a bad environment you will end up giving everything back. Why everything? Because historically, trending months are way shorter than non-trending periods. The market might trend for 2-3 months in a row and then go quiet for 3-4 months. In those 3-4 months, the action is so choppy — there is a saying: you don't die from 100 small cuts, but those small cuts can add up and really eat your account.

## The Position Sizing Calculator

I use a position sizing calculator that takes into account:
- Account size (the money you have)
- Risk percentage
- Stop loss distance

...and from those three inputs it determines the right number of shares to buy for every trade.

In the initial days — including me — we would just buy a round lot: 100 shares, 1000 shares, 500 shares. I'm sure you can relate to this. But it does not work that way. For example:

- Stock A: ₹500. You buy 1,000 shares. That's ₹5 lakhs invested.
- Stock B: ₹1,000. You buy 100 shares. That's ₹1 lakh invested.

Stock B's trade works and you make 10% on ₹1 lakh. Stock A's trade does not work and you lose 5% on ₹5 lakhs. Even though you made 10% on the winning trade, overall you're still in a loss — because on the bigger investment you lost more money.

## How the Calculator Works: Zen Technologies Example

This is my sheet — what I use every day. I'm going to share it with you. If you have a basic knowledge of Excel, you can make it yourself.

The columns are:
- **Net worth / account size** (the money you have)
- **Entry price**
- **Stop loss**
- **Risk %** (two columns: 0.5% and 1%)

- **Total investment amount** (auto-calculated)

Let me walk through the Zen Technologies (Corrected; raw: "Zentech") example:

- Entry price: **215**
- Stop: **2%** → 215 minus 2% = **210.7** (with charges and commission it comes to slightly above 2%)

Now the key distinction that beginners get confused about:

> **You are risking 0.5% of your account — not investing 0.5%.**

The risk % column is what you are risking, not what you are investing. With a 0.5% risk setting:
- You would buy **950 shares**
- You would be **investing approximately 20% of your account** in this one trade

If you take this trade, buy 950 shares, and tomorrow the stock hits your stop, you lose **0.5% of your account** — and that's it. You already know your worst-case scenario before entering. This takes away all the confusion and all the randomness from your trading.

## Solving the Gap-Down Problem With Math

How does this solve the gap-down problem? Simple math:

- Your stop is **2%**, and you are risking **0.5%** of your account.
- If the stock gaps down **10%** (5× your stop): you lose **2.5% of your account**.
- If the stock gaps down **20%** (10× your stop): you lose **5% of your account**.

Is 5% small? No, it is not small. But does it mean everything is over? No. You can always recover from 5%.

I've never had a 20% gap down in my years of trading — mainly because I trade only liquid stocks. When stocks are liquid and there is a gap down, there is always some buyer interested in that stock, so the price is stopped from crashing all the way. That's how you improve your odds of avoiding a 20% gap.

Very illiquid, micro-cap BSE [⚠ ASR garble: raw was "BSC" — likely "BSE" (exchange), or possibly "BSM"; context suggests illiquid small-caps] stocks are where 20% gap downs happen. If you trade only liquid stocks with proper (RVOL) dots, you will see there is less capital-wiping risk.

Let me give you a recent real example: I had a trade where my stop was 120 and the stock closed at 124. The next day it opened gap-down at 118. My stop was 120, the stock opened at 118. How much do I lose? At most half a percent twice — just 1% of my account. Do the math.

So with a 10% gap down, the maximum you can lose is **2.5% of your account**. That is something you can always recover from. That is how position sizing saves your account.

## How Much Should You Risk? Learning Phase vs. Running Phase

The two risk columns in my sheet are 0.5% and 1%. Here is how to use them by phase:

### Learning Phase (first 20-30 trades)
- **Start with a very low risk number** — even lower than 0.5%.
- I agree this is hardly any investment. I know that. But remember: in the learning phase you are going to make a lot of mistakes. Keep your mistakes smaller so you can survive the whole learning phase.
- I've seen people go down 50% in the learning phase, lose confidence, and never come back. That is what you should not do.
- Start with 1 [⚠ ASR garble: raw was "Start with 1" — context suggests "start with 0.1%" or a very small risk fraction, as Manas then says to not start with 0.5%; exact number unclear]. After 20-30 trades, review and check your mistake sheet.

### Running Phase (after proven consistency)
- Go to **0.5%**, or anywhere between **0.3% and 0.5%**.
- At 0.5% risk with a 20% account allocation per trade: if a trade goes up 50-60%, your account can grow really fast.
- This is where the real money is.

**The mistake sheet practice:** Every day, write down every mistake you made. With time, mistakes should get fewer. Writing has a different effect than just keeping things in your head — when you start monitoring and measuring something, it starts getting better because you're constantly reading and writing it.

## Key Takeaway

Position sizing eliminates randomness from your decisions:
- You always know your worst-case scenario before entering.
- No gap down can wipe you out if you are sized correctly.
- Your sizing is consistent and comparable across all your trades — you're not randomly buying 1,000 shares of one stock and 100 of another.
- When markets are good and you're making money, you can increase size. When markets are choppy, reduce it.

That is what I wanted to convey in this chapter on position sizing. That's how I eliminate the randomness from my decisions.
