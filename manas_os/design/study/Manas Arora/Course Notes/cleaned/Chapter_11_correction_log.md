## Important source facts preserved

- Drawdown means the account falling from a prior peak.
- Drawdowns cannot be escaped completely; every new and seasoned trader goes through them.
- The seasoned trader controls drawdown and knows where to slow down; the new trader keeps trading the same way.
- Behaviour changes in drawdown: execution, thinking, risk-taking and interference.
- Clear drawdown rules are mandatory.
- Example rule: after **3%** down from top, reduce trade size to half.
- Example: account reaches **₹10 lakh**, then drops **3%** from the top.
- Example risk reduction: from **1%** risk per trade to **0.5%**.
- If another **2%** is lost, making total drawdown **5%** from the top, suspend trading, close trades and take a break.
- For new traders with weak emotional control, Manas recommends a complete break from the market, not merely no trading while still watching every chart.
- Sit-out power is a skill.
- **5%**, **7%** and **3%** are treated as customizable example thresholds, not universal commandments.
- Drawdown baggage causes traders to interfere with valid trades.
- The stock does not know the trader's account status; analysis must stay neutral.
- During drawdowns, reduce risk size if needed, but do not automatically change execution or management if the market environment is still fine.
- The trader should analyse market conditions from the market's point of view, not from account P&L.
- Recovery from drawdown is not the real goal; taking good trades and making good money is the goal.
- Comeback process preserved: take very small-sized trades, let one or two work, then gradually increase if traction returns.
- Do not check account balance day to day because it attaches emotion to the number and changes execution.
- Strong belief comes only from taking trades, reviewing them and improving over months.
- Weight-loss analogy preserved: everyone knows the rules, but discipline is difficult.
- Self-monitoring rule preserved: be your own coach; no boss monitors trading behaviour.
- In rough patches, Manas takes a break and then trades very small because he is trying not to lose money, not trying to make money.
- Trailing example preserved: 20-moving-average line on closing basis + emergency stop; one close below the trailing line does not automatically mean exit if emergency stop is intact and reaction needs a day or two.
- Mechanical exit at **355** preserved as the example of what could happen if the 20-MA line is followed without reaction context.
- Final rules preserved: do not change execution/management style, do not watch balance daily, have clear rules for stop/reduce/comeback.

## Book-build caution

This chapter should become the drawdown operating protocol, not a vague resilience essay.

The book version must preserve the distinction:

- reduce **risk size** when drawdown rules trigger;
- stop trading when deeper drawdown rules trigger;
- but do not change valid execution/management purely because the account balance hurts.

This is also a bridge between Ch8 and Ch10:

- Ch8 teaches how not to interfere with a living trade.
- Ch10 teaches that behaviour is the edge.
- Ch11 shows what happens when drawdown pain attacks both.
