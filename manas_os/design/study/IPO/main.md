## Overview

This material teaches how to trade **freshly listed IPOs** (and similar small-base listings like BSC-to-NSC migrations) using a focused set of price action patterns. 

Key philosophy:
- IPO bases are small (often days to a few weeks), so **pattern + candlestick behavior** dominate over volume or complex fundamentals initially.
- Use **multi-timeframe analysis**: higher timeframe for context/validity, lower timeframes (75min, 15min) for precise low-risk entries with tight stops.
- "Combinations" matter: pattern + strength signs (higher lows, long tails, springs, no excessive retracement on strong candles) + right-side completion.
- IPOs have high upside potential because "nobody knows the value yet," so many setups are playable if the pattern appears cleanly.

The transcript repeatedly stresses:
- Adapt textbook patterns in real time (imperfect head sizes, shoulders, etc.).
- Focus on demand evidence and follow-through.
- Prioritize tight risk on fresh listings (avoid large stops).

## Core Concepts

**VCP (Volatility Contraction Pattern)** — Foundation
- Price range squeezes smaller and smaller (higher lows / contracting highs).
- Ideal: previous low defended while highs are tested or held (net buying by "institution").
- Higher Low = strength (institutions not letting it fall).
- Lower Low = weakness.
- Flat high + higher low = positive tilt.
- Lower high + higher low or flat low = weaker.
- Context always matters: candles inside the base, volume (less critical on tiny IPO bases), sector.

**Why IPOs are "pattern-heavy"**
- Bases are tiny → you have limited data → individual candles and simple structures carry more weight.
- Theme helps but is not required.
- Volume is secondary on very fresh bases ("you can't do volume analysis freshly").
- Potential is high because valuation discovery is just beginning.

**Multi-Timeframe Rule**
- Daily > 75min > 15min in importance.
- Always check higher TF shows strength or readiness before taking 15min setups.
- For most traders, stick to 15min and above; 1min increases frequency and whipsaw dramatically.

**Right-Side Completion (what to look for on the final contraction)**
- Inside bars (one candle inside another).
- Mini coils (multiple candles inside one prior candle's range).
- Coils / ranges.
- These are the triggers for entry on small IPO bases.

**Strength / Demand Signals (used across examples)**
- Long tail candles (small body, long lower tail) → demand pulling price up from below.
- Bottom spring + outside bar (down day followed by bar that engulfs prior close).
- Higher lows (especially multiple).
- Strong up candle with limited retracement (ideally <50% on the strong candle itself).
- No follow-through to the downside after a "dirty" candle during market weakness = shakeout.
- Dry volume on down days / consolidation; better volume on up days as base matures.

## Main Pattern Set (used repeatedly)

1. **Crowbar** — Strong candle followed by 2-3 small sideway candles, then resumption. Looks like a bar with a "crowbar" on top.
2. **Hook Setup** — Price pulls back toward EMA after breakout, retests area, then resumes. Price catches up to EMA.
3. **Fast Flag** — Breakout, then sideway/consolidation for a few days (price tries to catch EMA but doesn't fully), resumes. EMA catches up to price.
4. **Inverted Head & Shoulders (and on trendline)** — Common in these small bases. Real-time versions often have uneven shoulders/heads; use visual imagination.
5. **VCP / T-VCP / Tight Squeeze** — Contracting ranges, higher lows. Look for progressive tightening (e.g., 8pt → 5pt → 2pt ranges).
6. **Inside Bar / Mini Coil / Double Inside Bar** — Primary right-side triggers.
7. **Sideways / Range after breakout** — Treat as consolidation; trade the range break or coil inside it.

**Distinctions (speaker clarifies)**
- Hook: price comes back to EMA.
- Crowbar: price does not come all the way back; small candles sit and then it rips.
- Fast Flag: EMA catches up while price holds higher.

## Entry Tactics & Risk Management (from transcript)

- Prefer entries on first inside bar or first mini-coil after breakout where possible.
- On gap day / early: watch for inside bar or coil.
- Use lower TF for tighter stops (e.g., daily stop might be 3-4%, 75min or 15min can get 1.5-2%).
- Goal on fresh IPO: keep stops small (speaker tries not to exceed ~4% or so on very new names).
- Trail according to style and market: some hold to 20-40% on strong movers; half out at 15-20% is common suggestion.
- Shakeouts: one dirty candle in a strong stock during broad weakness is often buyable if no follow-through lower and prior low holds.
- Avoid or be very careful if:
  - Gap + OR extension too large on day 1 (risk of no quick risk-free).
  - Excessive retracement on strong candles (>50%).
  - Low liquidity chop (more whipsaws on lower TF).
  - No follow-through after breakout or after "good" candle.

**BSC-to-NSC listings**: Treated the same as IPOs behaviorally. Setup on NSC chart is tradable even if BSC has none.

## Key Rules & Heuristics Stated

- "IPO setups are pattern-heavy... as soon as you get the pattern you have to trade."
- "Candle size is more important than volume."
- "First inside bar after IPO-day breakout is often a good trigger."
- "50% retracement rule on strong candles": try to defend the 50% level of a strong up candle.
- "If it is showing strength (spring, higher low, long tail) then go to lower TF and trade the coil/inside bar."
- On real-time patterns: "Perfect patterns are rarely found... you have to make minor adjustments."
- "As base gets bigger, candle importance decreases, pattern + volume + theme importance increases."
- "For very small bases you can trade almost all of them because potential is high."
- Volume on first breakout less critical; volume on later legs and dry volume in consolidation becomes important as base ages.
- Speaker bias: prefers buying "higher" areas when possible to avoid time value of money decay from buying too low.
