## Overview

Tradetm is the trading education platform founded by **Anuragg Venkatakrishnan** (anuragg_ca), with contributions from **Chirag Kedia**. The content spans 2023–2026 and covers a comprehensive Indian momentum trading methodology built around:

1. **Episodic Pivots (EP)** as the highest-impact setup
2. **Position sizing and risk management** as the primary differentiator
3. **Market psychology** — anger, depression, tilt, Prospect Theory
4. **Situational awareness** — reading market phases, not just charts
5. **Process improvement** — feedback loops, journal analysis, bell curves
6. **Indian market adaptation** — illiquidity, circuit limits, small universe

The core philosophy: **Setups don't have an edge — the edge is in execution, context, and position sizing.** Markets reward those who understand the WHY behind price movement, not just the WHAT.

## Key Concepts and Principles

### 1. Episodic Pivots (EP) — The Highest-Impact Setup

EP is presented as the closest thing to a "Holy Grail" for Indian markets:
- New information (earnings, catalysts) surprises the market
- Stock must be neglected (base consolidation or downtrend)
- Gap-up + sustained follow-through driven by FOMO + institutional liquidity
- In India: success judged on follow-through (Day 1+), not just Day 0 gap

**EP advantages in India:**
- Scalability — volumes create liquidity, expanding tradeable universe
- Effort-to-reward — 15 minutes of tracking, execution in 9:07-9:30 AM window
- Execution improvement — pattern recognition, quick decisions, volatility management
- Market barometer — EP response reveals market's true risk appetite

**EP in bear markets:** EPs work even in bear markets because they represent fundamental shifts. The psychology of FOMO transcends market conditions. However, frequency drops when result quality deteriorates.

Sources: `On Bear Markets and EPs Explained_text.txt`, `Setup Prioritization and EPs_text.txt`, `Fundamentals and Themes_text.txt`

### 2. Position Sizing — The Primary Differentiator

**Core principle:** Position size is a derivative of % risk and stop loss, NOT a static percentage of portfolio.

**Key rules:**
- Risk 0.5-0.65% per trade maximum
- Total open risk never exceeds 4-5% at any time
- Avoid initiating more than 3-4 positions simultaneously (velocity/hybrid trades)
- Tight SL (1-2.5%) enables larger position sizes and faster R-multiples
- Sell 30-50% in strength at 4R, trail the rest

**MTF (Margin Trading Facility):**
- MTF funds capital, not risk — position size calculated on base capital
- 5× leverage means blocking ₹50K for a ₹2.5L position
- Risk remains on ₹10L portfolio, not the leveraged amount
- Maximum ~200% exposure through 4-5 positions

**Case study (JBMA):**
- Tight SL (2%): 25% size → 60.7R impact on portfolio (30.4%)
- Moderate SL (5%): 12.5% size → 23.6R impact (11.8%)
- Wide SL (8%): 6.25% size → 14.5R impact (7.25%)
- Same stock, same move — tighter SL + larger size = 4x portfolio impact

Sources: `Position Sizing_text.txt`, `Complete Guide to MTF_text.txt`

### 3. Market Psychology — Anger, Depression, and Prospect Theory

**Prospect Theory (Kahneman & Tversky):** Losses have greater emotional impact than equivalent gains. Even with 35-65% win rate, the net emotional impact is negative.

**Tilt mapping framework:**
1. What's the problem: Unrealized profits feel like earned money
2. Why it exists: Utility value attachment (EMI, rent, vacation)
3. What's flawed: Trading goal is generational wealth, not covering expenses
4. Correction: Realized profit is the only profit that matters
5. Logic confirmation: You didn't learn to drive just to stay in second gear

**Key insight:** "Anger arises when underlying flaws conflict with reality. Every elite performer has used anger as a core trigger to channel performance."

Sources: `Anger and Depression_text.txt`

### 4. Situational Awareness — Reading Market Phases

**Four market phases:**
1. Demand Domination — significant buying activity
2. Supply Domination — selling pressure dominant
3. Lack of Demand — buying exhausts, no new demand
4. Lack of Supply — selling exhausts, no further supply

**Market type framework (Dr. Van Tharp):**
- Bull Normal, Bull Volatile
- Bear Normal, Bear Volatile
- Sideways Quiet, Sideways Volatile

**For long-only traders, only Bear Volatile is truly "dead."** All other conditions offer tradeable opportunities.

**Execution adaptation per phase:**
- EP pullbacks and high RS stocks work in lack-of-demand
- Shorts available when 50%+ of small/mid-cap universe is Stage 4
- Parabolic longs from oversold bounces
- IPOs offer fresh narratives unburdened by market history

Sources: `Situational Awareness_text.txt`, `On Bear Markets_text.txt`

### 5. Process Improvement — The Bell Curve and Inchworm

**Decision Bell Curve:**
- Every trader has a natural range (A-game to C-game)
- A-game: peak conscious competence, learning mistakes
- C-game: known-known mistakes from emotional excesses
- B-game: tactical errors between extremes

**Inchworm concept:**
- Improve A-game (one step forward from front)
- Improve C-game (one step forward from back)
- Over time, entire bell curve shifts right
- Previous B-game becomes new C-game; previous A-game becomes new B-game

**Common mistake:** Focusing only on strengths (A-game) widens the bell curve. "Your trading system is only as strong as its weakest link."

Sources: `Improving Trading Processes_text.txt`

### 6. Feedback Loops — Build→Measure→Learn

**Build with frequency and speed:**
- Take many trades initially, prioritize quantity over perfection
- Start with velocity trades (VCP, breakouts) for faster feedback
- Shorter feedback loops = easier replanning

**Measure tangibles and intangibles:**
- Trade journal is a tool, not a spreadsheet
- Tangibles: journal ratios, monthly summaries, equity curve
- Intangibles: questions asked during post-review

**Key journal insights (from author's experience):**
- "Could I have reduced my SL?" → In 85% of cases, price dropping below 3% would trigger 8% SL anyway. Tighter SL = 71% more returns.
- "Could I have entered earlier?" → 55% of winners didn't immediately become risk-free. Started buying closer to momentum start (previous day high → opening range high).
- "What is the cause of the move?" → 70% of biggest winners started with gap-ups from surprise earnings → shifted capital to EP setups.

**Naval quote:** "It isn't 10,000 hours that creates outliers, it's 10,000 iterations."

Sources: `Developing Feedback Loops_text.txt`

### 7. Entry Frameworks — Strategic Thinking Over Tactics

**Three learning parts:**
1. Beginner's theory — what is VCP, what are entries
2. Tactical part — how to execute (tight stops, entries, pyramiding)
3. Strategic thinking — the most neglected, highest-impact part

**Why strategic thinking matters:**
- Tactical without strategic = no use
- Chess analogy: each move is a resource to create permanent weakness in opponent
- Qullamaggie's edge: not WHAT he bought, but HOW he placed himself strategically
- "You don't trade charts, you trade your P&L"

**Supply concepts:**
- **Built-up supply:** Develops from stock appreciation — holders want to sell but fear missing out
- **Overhead supply:** Develops from correction — holders underwater, sell on any bounce
- Key: "99% of people try to identify structure in ranges — structure doesn't matter in ranges"

Sources: `entry_framework_formatted.txt`

### 8. Trading Intuition Over Objective Rules

**Plato's Allegory of the Cave applied to trading:**
- Charts, indicators, fundamentals are shadows — not the price movement itself
- Price moves as collective result of all participants' sentiment
- The only utility of analysis is to sync with participants generating net order flows

**Why intuition matters:**
- Markets are emotional creatures — rigid rules cannot govern trader decisions
- "The exceptions themselves vary" — why algorithmic systems underperform
- Need for objectivity stemmed from need for perfection/control
- "Subjectivity is necessary for improving objectivity"

**How to develop intuition:**
- Trial and error + granular post-trade reviews
- Question discipline when intuition contradicts rules
- "It is sometimes easier to begin with the comfort of a lie rather than burden your progress with perceived realities"

Sources: `Trading Intuition Over Objective Rules_text.txt`

### 9. Indian Market Adaptations

**Illiquidity costs (Prof. Damodaran framework):**
1. **Bid-ask spread:** Small caps can have 6.55% spread vs 0.52% for large caps
2. **Price impact:** Large trades create imbalance → erratic spikes/shakeouts
3. **Opportunity cost of waiting:** Splitting blocks = higher entry prices

**Execution adaptations:**
- Add 0.3-0.6% buffer for slippages even with tight SLs
- Never use market orders — identify "obvious" order clusters and execute before
- When trailing SL is far, set alerts instead of system stops
- Once portfolio exceeds ₹1Cr, shift from velocity to positional strategies
- Prioritize IPOs and EPs — they naturally attract liquidity

**9-Million scan adaptation:**
- Static volume numbers fail in India (too many penny stocks with high volume)
- Solution: Relative Volume (RVOL) > 6 replaces the 9M filter
- RVOL adapts to every stock regardless of price/liquidity

Sources: `The Cost of Illiquidity_text.txt`, `9 mil vol scan_text.txt`

### 10. The Swing Trader Fallacy

**Portfolio return equation:** Expectancy × Frequency

**The Cycle problem:**
1. Full cash during correction
2. 5-15 days redeploying as setups form
3. Few days of upswing → pullback triggers SLs → back to cash
4. Repeat

**Result:** ~50% effective capital utilization, never building magnitude moves or executing enough trades for super performance.

**Solution:** Lean towards one style — either more day trader or more positional trader. "Trying to find equilibrium across market cycles is a tricky, high-effort, low-reward game."

**Minervini example:** Won USIC 2021 with 334.8% by adapting to short-term 1-5 day trades when breakouts lacked follow-through.

Sources: `Trade the Market You are in_text.txt`

## Trading Rules and Frameworks

### Entry Rules
- Buy when stock is fresh (0-20% from base breakout), not when already up 60%+
- Buy pullbacks to 10/21 EMA in trending stocks
- Buy ORB highs or previous day highs for tighter SL
- EP: Sort gap-ups by %, enter on 1/3/5 min ORB high, SL = day low
- D2 Entry: Scan day's top gainers, prefer 10%+ moves, enter on Day 2 intraday structure

### Exit Rules
- Sell 30-50% in strength at 4R (makes trade risk-free)
- Trail remaining with 21 EMA (daily) or 10 EMA (intraday)
- Exit if previous day low broken within parabolic move
- EP: "Sell in weakness" — exit if 21 EMA breached
- For persistent momentum: trail 50 DMA, accept giving back some profit

### Risk Management Rules
- Max 0.5-0.65% risk per trade
- Max 3-4 open positions (velocity/hybrid)
- Total open risk ≤ 4-5%
- Always have SL in system — "no mental stop-loss"
- Never trade with borrowed money
- 3% hard drawdown limit before stopping

### Position Sizing Formula
```
Position Size = Portfolio Risk ($) / (Entry - Stop Loss)
Example: ₹10L portfolio, 0.5% risk = ₹5,000
Entry ₹100, SL ₹98 → 2,500 shares = ₹2.5L (25% of portfolio)
```
