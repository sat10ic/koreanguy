## Overview

This collection is a focused study on **Episodic Pivots (EP)** adapted specifically for Indian markets (circuit limits, post-market earnings, no off-hours trading).

EP is presented as the "ultimate lovechild of a trader and a fundamental investor": a high effort-to-reward setup that gets the trader in close to the origin of a major narrative shift.

Core idea:
- New information (primarily strong post-close earnings or non-earnings catalysts) surprises the market.
- Stock is "neglected" (base consolidation or downtrend).
- Results in gap-up / strong open + sustained move driven by FOMO + institutional liquidity.
- In India: success is judged on **follow-through** (especially Day 1+), not just Day 0 emotional gap.

Claimed edge: ~10-20 strong EPs/year; historically <10% of trades but >35% of returns (from deep dive analysis of 1688 gap-ups 2017-2022).

## Key Concepts and Definitions

**Episodic Pivot (EP)**: A shift in narrative or trend where new information forces revaluation. "It is a new episode in the life of the company and its share price." (guide files)

Differentiated from plain "earnings gap up": context and narrative change matter more than the gap itself.

**Two types**:
- **Earnings EP**: Strong YoY + QoQ growth (Stock Bee rule: 30%+ in both sales and EPS). Focus on after-market close reports.
- **Non-Earnings EP**: Indirect earnings impact — new orders (RVNL, JBMA, Anuras), govt policies (sugar, fertiliser), etc.

**Neglected stock**: "No one is putting money... a dead stock" or range-bound with no trend interest. Range can form from equal effort or zero effort. Low float + neglected = bigger magnitude moves. Consolidation bases outperform pure downtrends.

**Day 0 vs Follow-through**:
- Day 0 often emotional (institutions + retail FOMO).
- "EP success is NOT based on your ORB entry. It is based on the follow through."
- Many Day 0 gaps fail or pull back hard on Day 1. True test starts Day 1-2+.
- Management commentary after results often provides the real fuel (future projections, capex, contracts).

**IPO + EP special case**: New listings are under-tracked. IPO + base breakout + strong result = "purple spot". Less people have positions; confirmation can trigger strong follow-on.

## How to Identify EP (from Guide + QnA)

From the written playbook:
- Post-market results analysis: look for unexpected positive growth (30%+ benchmark, not strict).
- Bullish earnings presentation helps (example: NDTV Feb 2022).
- **Must** gap up or open strongly next day — otherwise market not surprised.
- Stock must be neglected (big base or downtrend). Filter market cap > ~300 Cr.
- Do **not** rely on volume early (buy before it shows).
- Charts must show "something significant has changed."

From QnA elaboration:
- Prioritize momentum bursts that already have catalyst + liquidity flow before the move.
- 80% of the time results are "not strong enough" — you are playing technical action + context.
- Track a list of everything that gapped up and started failing (potential later recovery plays?).
- Strong catalysts (govt policy, big tie-ups like Nvidia) make moves more resilient even in bad market context.
- Earnings after very late hours (9-10pm) mean less immediate smart money can act next morning.

## How to Buy / Entry Tactics

**On gap day (Day 0)**:
- 5-min opening range breakout high, stop = day's low (often breakout bar low).
- Skip if gap + OR high extension > ~12% (circuit limits prevent quick risk-free).
- Less than 45% of EPs trigger on gap day per deep dive; those that do often give immediate risk-free entries for pyramiding.
- Win rate observed 40-60%, initial stops 2-4%.
- Common pattern: tight/negative bar close near MAs, entry near previous high on strong start, SL near day low.

**Pullback entries**:
- Most EPs offer pullback around 10/21 EMA.
- High R:R, good for stocks where earnings "not great" but reaction was.
- Entry near prior day high on strong start after pullback.

**Late play / follow-through**:
- You can enter on follow-up strength if management commentary validates narrative.
- But "when you are late play... it does not mean that every delayed DP ... is going to be good."
- Put the delayed entry in context of the result + follow-up info.

**Risk management notes**:
- Goal: trade becomes risk-free quickly due to urgency of setup.
- If it stalls or takes too long → higher failure probability.

## How to Sell / Exit

- **Primary**: Sell in weakness. "EP is a primary case for selling in weakness."
- Trail: Exit if 21 EMA breached (low of bar closing below broken). Or 50 DMA if distance not too large.
- Hold "all or nothing" on the strong ones — better results in the deep dive.
- Only sell into strength on temporary extensions (~15% from 10 EMA) or to maintain mental capital (plan to buy back).
- Pyramiding: every pullback to 10/21 EMA is an add opportunity.

Statistics cited:
- 10-20 strong EPs per year expected.
- EPs <10% of trades → >35% of returns (last 2 years in the referenced period).
- "Balancing trading with life becomes easier when 10% of trades generate 35% of returns."

## Q&A Highlights & Concrete Examples

**Ola (and similar)**:
- Result during market hours → hit upper circuit same day, then faded.
- Later management commentary on production, capex, utilization → move resumed.
- Lesson: participants wait for management commentary on future results, not just past numbers. Pure price action entry still valid but context improves conviction.

**Netweb (IPO EP example)**:
- Gap up, failed initially.
- Later Nvidia tie-up commentary created the real gap-up context.
- "I just played it on price action" initially.
- IPO + result context can be powerful because less pre-existing holders.

**Angel (example of failure after making risk-free)**:
- Strong Day 0.
- Then pulled back; credit access issues surfaced days later.
- "There are so many stock outs which happen after making your risk free."

**Other mentions**: Power India (hypothetical), MSDC, Alex Lakshmi Chem, Ren Industries (earnings but no positive reaction yet), National Aluminium (sector catalyst).

**Neglected identification**:
- Price action that is not moving either side (trendless range).
- "Dead stock is a neglect."
- Simple: anything not in an uptrend.
- For 5-year old answers: stocks 100%+ up in last 6 months are not neglected.

**Market environment**:
- Strong catalysts make EPs more resilient to broad market fluctuations.
- In poor market periods (e.g. Oct quarter), earnings-driven EPs were the only ones that worked for the speaker.
- "The only stocks that actually worked during those times were the earnings moves."

**India-specific**:
- Very few "core" compounders; EPs rarer than US.
- Diluting concept to Indian context: earnings often replace pure episodic paper.
- Regulatory/govt policy has oversized impact (especially commodities, manufacturing bans).
- SEBI relaxations mean IPO companies sometimes delay results → timing friction.
